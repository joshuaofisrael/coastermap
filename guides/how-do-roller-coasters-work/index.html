How do roller coasters work?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: a lift hill or launch gives the train its energy at the start, and gravity does the rest. On each drop the train turns height into speed, and on each hill speed back into height, losing a little to friction each time, which is why the first drop is the biggest.

Energy in, energy out

A traditional roller coaster has no engine on the train. A lift hill or a launch gives the train energy at the start of the ride, and the rest of the circuit spends it. At the top of the lift the train has the most potential energy; as it drops, that becomes kinetic energy, or speed. Every following hill trades some speed back for height, and friction from the wheels and air resistance slowly drain the total. That is why the first drop is almost always the biggest and the hills get smaller as the ride goes on.

Lift hills

Most coasters use a chain lift: a moving chain under the track that a "chain dog" on the bottom of the train catches, pulling the train up the slope. The clacking sound is the anti-rollback device, a ratchet that stops the train rolling backwards if it ever stops on the lift. Some rides use a cable lift, which is faster and quieter, or a tyre-driven lift on smaller rides. Of the 1131 coasters on Coaster Map, 75 have a launch model on RCDB and 1056 do not.

Launches

A launch replaces the lift with a burst of acceleration. See how roller coaster launches work.

Staying on the track and going upside down

Coaster wheels clamp the rail from three sides, so the train cannot lift off even when riders are upside down or floating. See how roller coasters stay on the track.

Blocks and brakes

The track is divided into blocks, and only one train is allowed in each block at a time. Brakes at the end of each block stop a train if the block ahead is not clear, so trains cannot catch each other. See how roller coaster brakes work and what happens if a roller coaster gets stuck.

Frequently asked questions

How do roller coasters work?

A lift hill or launch gives the train energy at the start, and gravity does the rest. The train turns height into speed on each drop and speed back into height on each hill, slowly losing energy to friction, so later hills are smaller.

Do roller coasters have engines?

Not on the train in a traditional coaster. Motors drive the lift chain, cable or launch system, and the train coasts for the rest of the ride.

What is the clicking sound on a lift hill?

The anti-rollback device: a ratchet that stops the train rolling backwards if it ever stops on the lift.

Why is the first hill the biggest?

The train loses energy to friction and air resistance as it goes, so it cannot climb back to the height it started from.
