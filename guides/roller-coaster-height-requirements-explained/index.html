Roller coaster height requirements explained

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: parks measure riders at each ride entrance with a measuring stick and enforce the posted number strictly, even for a fraction of an inch. Many rides also have a lower height range that can ride with a supervising companion.

How parks measure

Parks measure riders at the ride, not at the gate. Walt Disney World's official planDisney panel says children "can be measured at the entrance to each of the attractions", that Cast Members at rides with height restrictions have measuring sticks there, and that height restrictions are strictly enforced "even if your child is only off by a teensy-weensie amount". Another planDisney answer says there is no central measuring location: there is typically a measurement spot at the queue entrance "and possibly again right before entering the attraction itself", so a child who only just passes may be checked twice.

Universal Orlando's official rider guide says "Shoes and shirts must be worn at all times", so plan on your child wearing shoes both when measured and when riding, and measure at home in the same shoes. Do not count on a hairstyle or hat to add height, and expect a check at each ride.

If your child is near the line

  • Measure at home against a wall, standing straight with heels flat, in the shoes they will wear. Round down, not up.
  • Ask staff to measure at the entrance before you join a long queue.
  • Have a plan B nearby, such as a family coaster with a lower requirement. Our height checker shows what a given height can ride at every park we cover.
  • Ask about rider swap (also called child swap or rider switch) where the park offers it, so adults can take turns without queuing twice.

Ride-alone minimum vs companion range

Many rides have two numbers. The ride-alone minimum is the height to ride without an adult; the lower companion range means the child can ride only with a supervising companion. Universal Orlando defines a Supervising Companion as "someone who is 14 years of age or older and meets all other attraction ridership requirements", for children under its 48 in (122 cm) ride-alone height. Of the 1345 coasters on Coaster Map with a verified height rule, 525 publish a companion range. Companion rules differ by park, so check each ride's page.

Why height requirements exist

A rider has to be tall enough for the restraint to hold them properly and for their body to be braced against the ride's forces. Universal Orlando's guide says that for most rides you "must be able to independently maintain an upright position, and brace and support your torso, neck, and head while absorbing sudden and dramatic movements". That is why the number is fixed per ride, and why some rides also have a maximum height. See can you be too tall for a roller coaster?

The most common height requirements

Across the coasters we cover, the most common ride-alone minimums at parks that publish inches are 48 in (275 coasters), 54 in (85 coasters), 42 in (54 coasters), 52 in (42 coasters), 36 in (22 coasters). At parks that publish centimetres they are 120 cm (265 coasters), 130 cm (183 coasters), 140 cm (126 coasters), 110 cm (50 coasters), 90 cm (24 coasters).

Frequently asked questions

How do theme parks measure height for rides?

At each ride entrance with a measuring stick, and sometimes again before boarding. Walt Disney World's official planDisney panel says Cast Members at rides with height restrictions have measuring sticks at the entrances.

Do kids get measured with shoes on?

Plan on it. Universal Orlando's official rider guide says shoes must be worn at all times, so children wear shoes both when measured and when riding. Measure at home in the same shoes.

Can my child ride if they are half an inch short?

No. Walt Disney World's planDisney panel says height restrictions are strictly enforced even for a tiny difference, and other parks apply their posted minimums too.

What is a supervising companion?

At Universal Orlando, someone 14 or older who meets all other ride requirements and rides with a child under the 48 in ride-alone height. Other parks set their own rules.
