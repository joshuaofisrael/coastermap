Roller coaster types explained

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Roller coasters are grouped by what the track is made of, how the train gets its speed, and how riders sit. This page links our plain-English explainer for each type, with every example we cover and its height requirement.

Track material

Wooden coasters run on laminated wood track and feel loose and lively; steel coasters run on tubular or box steel track and allow smooth inversions; hybrids mix the two.

How the ride starts

Most coasters use a chain or cable lift hill. Launch coasters fire the train along the track instead, and shuttle coasters run the course forwards and then backwards.

How you sit

Sit-down coasters are the most common. On inverted coasters you hang below the track, on wing coasters you sit beside it, on flying coasters you lie face down, and on spinning coasters your car rotates.

Frequently asked questions

What are the main types of roller coaster?

By material: wooden, steel and hybrid. By how they start: lift hill, launch or shuttle. By seating: sit-down, inverted, wing, flying, floorless, stand-up, dive and spinning. Size classes include hyper (200 ft or more), giga (300 ft or more) and strata (400 ft or more) coasters.

Which type of roller coaster is the least intense?

Family coasters and mine trains, which usually have no inversions and low height requirements.
