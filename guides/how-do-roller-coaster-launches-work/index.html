How do roller coaster launches work?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: a launch replaces the lift hill with a burst of acceleration. Most use electromagnets along the track (LSM or LIM), a hydraulic motor pulling a cable, compressed air or spinning tyres to push the train up to speed in a few seconds.

The main launch systems

  • Linear synchronous motors (LSM): rows of electromagnets along the track switch on and off in sequence, pulling magnets fixed under the train forward. LSM launches are smooth, precise and can be repeated several times in one ride.
  • Linear induction motors (LIM): a similar idea, where electromagnets induce a current in a metal fin under the train, pushing it along. LIMs were common on early launch coasters.
  • Hydraulic launches: hydraulic motors wind a cable onto a drum very quickly. The cable pulls a catch car that hooks the train, accelerates it and lets go. This system has powered many of the fastest coasters in the world.
  • Pneumatic (compressed air) launches: compressed air drives the train or a catch car, giving very sudden acceleration.
  • Tyre launches: rows of spinning tyres grip a fin under the train, the same way tyres drive trains up many lift hills. Common on family launch coasters.
  • Flywheel and weight-drop catapults: older designs that store energy in a spinning flywheel or a falling weight and release it into a cable.

What a launch feels like

Instead of the slow build-up of a lift hill, you are pushed back into your seat as the train accelerates, often reaching highway speeds in a few seconds. Some coasters launch more than once, launch backwards, or launch up a tall "top hat" tower. Many nervous riders find a launch easier than a big lift hill because there is no time to dwell; others find the acceleration the most intense part of any ride.

Why launches sometimes roll back

On a launch up a tall tower, the train needs just enough speed to clear the top. In some conditions, or with a lightly loaded train, it can fall short and roll back down to the launch track, where brakes stop it. Rides that can roll back are designed for it.

Frequently asked questions

How do roller coaster launches work?

Most use linear synchronous or induction motors (electromagnets along the track), hydraulic motors pulling a cable, compressed air or spinning tyres to push the train from low speed to high speed in seconds.

What is an LSM launch?

A launch using linear synchronous motors: electromagnets along the track switch on in sequence and pull magnets under the train forward.

What is a rollback on a launch coaster?

When a train launched up a tall tower does not quite reach the top and rolls back to the launch track, where brakes stop it. Rides that can roll back are designed for it.

Are launch coasters scarier than lift hill coasters?

It depends on the rider. There is no slow climb to build anticipation, but the acceleration is strong.
