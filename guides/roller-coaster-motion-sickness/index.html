Roller coaster motion sickness: causes and tips

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: motion sickness on coasters comes from your eyes and inner ear disagreeing, especially on spinning, backwards and dark rides. Sit in the middle facing forward, look ahead, stay hydrated and take breaks. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine.

General information only. This page summarises public health guidance and park safety rules. It is not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine, and follow the warning signs at each ride.

Why coasters can make you feel sick

Motion sickness happens when the signals from your eyes, inner ear and body do not match. Coasters with spinning cars, backwards sections, dark indoor sections or screens are common triggers, because what you see and what you feel disagree. Universal Orlando's warning symbols include "Do not ride if you are susceptible to motion sickness or dizziness" on rides where that applies.

What public health guidance says

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) travel guidance on motion sickness suggests, among other steps, looking at the horizon, staying hydrated, limiting alcoholic and caffeinated drinks, eating small amounts of food frequently, avoiding smoking and distracting yourself, for example with music. The CDC Yellow Book says habituation, gradual repeated exposure to the motion, is the most effective countermeasure, that sleep deprivation can make motion sickness worse, and that medicine is most effective when taken before the motion starts rather than after symptoms begin. The CDC notes many motion sickness medicines cause drowsiness and says to talk to a healthcare professional about whether to use them.

Practical tips at the park

  • Sit in the middle of the train and face forward. Look ahead, not at the floor.
  • Take breaks between rides and drink water.
  • Skip spinning coasters and shuttle coasters, which run backwards, if they trouble you.
  • Build up gradually across the day, starting with a gentle coaster.

Frequently asked questions

How do you avoid motion sickness on roller coasters?

Sit in the middle facing forward, look ahead, avoid spinning and backwards rides if they trouble you, stay hydrated and take breaks. CDC guidance adds looking at the horizon and limiting alcohol and caffeine. Ask a doctor or pharmacist about medicine.

Why do roller coasters make me sick?

Motion sickness happens when your eyes and inner ear send conflicting signals, which is common on spinning, backwards and dark rides.

Can you take motion sickness medicine before a theme park?

The CDC says medicine is most effective before the motion starts and that many such medicines cause drowsiness. Talk to a healthcare professional before using any medicine.
