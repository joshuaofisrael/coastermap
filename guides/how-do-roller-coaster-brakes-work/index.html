How do roller coaster brakes work?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: the brakes are on the track, not the train. Friction brakes squeeze a fin under the train and can hold it still; magnetic brakes slow the fin with eddy currents, with no contact and no power. Block brakes keep every train in its own section of track.

Two kinds of brakes

  • Friction brakes are pairs of pads, a bit like a giant bicycle brake, that squeeze a metal fin under the train. They can hold a train completely still, so they are used where the train has to stop, such as the station and block brakes, and they are usually designed to close if power or air pressure is lost.
  • Magnetic brakes use rows of strong permanent magnets. As a metal fin on the train passes between them, it creates eddy currents that resist the motion and slow the train smoothly, with no contact and little wear. Because they rely only on the motion of the train, they work without power. They slow a train but cannot hold it at a standstill, so they are combined with friction brakes or tyres for the final stop.

Trim brakes, block brakes and the brake run

Trim brakes sit partway through a course and take a little speed off, so a ride runs within its design speed. Block brakes divide the track into sections; a train is only allowed into the next block when it is clear, so two trains can never meet. The final brake run brings the train down to walking pace before the station, where tyres usually move it into place.

Are the brakes on the train?

On nearly all coasters, the brakes are on the track and the train carries only the fins they grip. That way the ride's control system, not each train, decides when to slow or stop.

Shuttle coasters and brakes

Shuttle coasters, which run their course forwards and then backwards, rely on brakes and tyres at both ends. Examples we cover:

Frequently asked questions

How do roller coaster brakes work?

Most coasters use friction brakes, where pads squeeze a fin under the train, and magnetic brakes, where magnets slow a metal fin through eddy currents without touching it. The brakes are on the track, not the train.

How do magnetic brakes work on a roller coaster?

A metal fin on the train passes between strong permanent magnets. The motion creates eddy currents that resist the fin and slow the train smoothly, with no contact and no power needed.

Do roller coasters have brakes?

Yes, on the track: block brakes, trim brakes and a final brake run, managed by the ride's control system.
