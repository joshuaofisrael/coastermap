How to not be scared of roller coasters

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: start with a gentle family coaster with no inversions, sit in the middle of the train, breathe out on the drops and keep your head back. Step up one level at a time; the second ride on any coaster is easier than the first.

Why roller coasters feel scary

Fear on a coaster usually comes from three things: the anticipation in the queue and on the lift hill, the feeling of falling on the drops, and a sense of not being in control. Your body reacts to a drop the same way it would to a real fall, with a rush of adrenaline, a racing heart and the familiar "stomach drop", even though you are held in a seat designed to keep you in place through every part of the ride.

Knowing what is coming takes away a lot of that fear. Watch the ride run a few times from the queue, read the ride's page so you know whether it has inversions, launches or a big drop, and pick your seat on purpose.

Ten tips that actually help

  1. Start small. Ride a family coaster with no inversions first, then step up one level at a time. A ladder of rides builds confidence far better than jumping straight onto the biggest coaster in the park.
  2. Sit in the middle. The middle of the train feels the gentlest on most coasters. The front gives you the full view of every drop, and the back is pulled over the top of hills fastest.
  3. Breathe out, or shout, on the drops. Holding your breath tightens your stomach and makes the drop feel worse. A long exhale or a good scream relaxes your core.
  4. Keep your head back and your body relaxed. Rest your head against the headrest where there is one, sit up straight and hold the lap bar or grab handles.
  5. Look ahead, not down. Fixing your eyes on the track ahead or the horizon helps your brain predict the motion.
  6. Ride in daylight. Dark rides and night rides feel faster because you cannot see what is coming.
  7. Choose a short queue. A long wait gives anxiety time to build.
  8. Ride with someone calm. A friend who has ridden before can tell you what to expect, element by element.
  9. Ride it twice. The second ride is almost always less scary, because you know what is coming.
  10. You can step out. If you change your mind in the queue, tell a team member. Many coasters have an exit before the boarding platform.

Fear of drops, loops or launches

If drops are the problem, a launch coaster can be easier than a big lift hill, because there is no slow climb and the launch is over in seconds. If loops are the worry, it may help to know that on most inversions you are pressed into your seat rather than hanging, so it rarely feels like being upside down. Frequently asked questions

How do I stop being scared of roller coasters?

Start with a gentle family coaster, sit in the middle of the train, breathe out on the drops, and step up one level at a time. Riding the same coaster twice also helps because the second ride is less of a surprise.

Which seat is least scary on a roller coaster?

The middle of the train. The front shows you every drop and the back is pulled over hills fastest, so the middle usually feels the gentlest.

Is it normal to be scared of roller coasters?

Yes. A drop triggers the same adrenaline response as a real fall. Many keen riders were nervous at first and built up gradually.

Can I leave the queue if I change my mind?

Yes. Tell a team member. Many coasters have an exit before the boarding platform.
