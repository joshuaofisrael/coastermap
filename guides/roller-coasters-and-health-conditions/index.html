Roller coasters, pregnancy and health conditions: what parks say

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: parks warn expectant mothers and riders with heart conditions, abnormal blood pressure, back or neck problems, or unhealed surgery not to ride many coasters. This is general information only: follow the warning sign at each ride and your doctor's advice.

General information only. This page quotes official park safety rules. It is not medical advice. If you have a health condition or are pregnant, talk to your doctor before your visit and always follow the warning signs at each ride and the instructions of ride staff.

What parks say

Parks post health warnings at ride entrances, and the warnings differ from ride to ride. Universal Orlando's official Guide for Rider Safety and Accessibility is a clear example. Its warning symbols include:

  • "Do not ride if you have a history of heart conditions or abnormal blood pressure."
  • "Do not ride if you have back, neck, or similar physical conditions."
  • "Do not ride if you have unhealed surgery or fractures."
  • "Do not ride if you have weakened bones or muscles."
  • "Do not ride if you are an expectant mother."
  • "Do not ride if you are susceptible to motion sickness or dizziness."
  • "Do not ride if you have other conditions which may be aggravated by this ride."

The same guide says: "You know your physical conditions and limitations; Universal Orlando does not. If you suspect your health could be at risk for any reason, or you could aggravate a pre-existing condition of any kind, do not experience the attraction." It also says that for most rides you "must be able to independently maintain an upright position, and brace and support your torso, neck, and head while absorbing sudden and dramatic movements throughout the experience."

Pregnancy

Universal Orlando's symbol for expectant mothers ("Do not ride if you are an expectant mother") appears on the rides where it applies, and other parks post their own warnings at ride entrances. Check the sign at each ride and ask your doctor.

Heart, blood pressure, back and neck

Coasters subject riders to sudden acceleration, braking and changes in direction, which is why parks warn riders with heart conditions, abnormal blood pressure, back or neck conditions, or unhealed surgery or fractures. On Coaster Map, each coaster page shows RCDB's intensity class and, where the park publishes it, the park's own thrill rating, but these are not a substitute for the park's warning sign or a doctor's advice.

Before you go

  • Read the park's rider safety or accessibility guide for each ride you plan to ride.
  • Talk to your doctor if you have any condition on the warning lists.
  • At the park, read the sign at each ride entrance and ask staff if anything is unclear.

Frequently asked questions

Can you ride roller coasters while pregnant?

Parks warn against it. Universal Orlando's official rider guide, for example, uses the warning "Do not ride if you are an expectant mother" on rides where it applies. Follow each ride's sign and your doctor's advice.

Can you ride roller coasters with a heart condition?

Parks warn against it on many rides. Universal Orlando's guide says "Do not ride if you have a history of heart conditions or abnormal blood pressure." Talk to your doctor and follow each ride's sign.

Can you ride roller coasters with back problems?

Many rides carry a warning such as Universal Orlando's "Do not ride if you have back, neck, or similar physical conditions." Follow each ride's sign and your doctor's advice.
