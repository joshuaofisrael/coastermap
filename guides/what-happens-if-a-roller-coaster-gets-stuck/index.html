What happens if a roller coaster gets stuck?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: the ride's control system stops trains at safe points, on brakes or the lift hill, where they are held in place. Staff then restart the ride or walk riders off using stairs and walkways. Stay seated and wait for instructions.

Why coasters stop

A roller coaster's control system watches sensors all along the track. If anything is outside the expected range, such as a train running slower than it should, a sensor not reporting, high wind or a problem in the station, the system stops trains at the next safe point. Stopping is the designed response to a fault, not the fault itself.

Where a train stops

The track is divided into blocks separated by brakes, and only one train is allowed in a block at a time. When the system stops, trains halt on block brakes or the lift hill, so they cannot reach each other. On a lift hill, the anti-rollback ratchet holds the train in place. Coasters are designed with enough speed through every inversion, so stops happen on brakes and lifts rather than upside down.

What happens next

Usually the ride is restarted after staff check the system, and trains continue to the station. If not, staff walk riders off the train. Universal Orlando's official rider guide warns: "If an evacuation of an attraction or venue is required, Guests may be required to navigate stairs, narrow walkways, or ladders." It adds: "Please have a member of your party assist you, or wait in the ride vehicle for assistance."

If you are on a stopped coaster

  • Stay seated with your restraint locked.
  • Keep hands, feet and belongings inside the car.
  • Wait for instructions from ride staff, and do not try to climb out.
  • If you have a medical need, tell staff when they reach you.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if a roller coaster gets stuck?

Trains stop on block brakes or the lift hill, where they are held in place. Staff restart the ride or walk riders off using stairs and walkways.

Can a roller coaster stop upside down?

Coasters are designed with enough speed through each inversion, and stops happen on brakes or lift hills rather than in loops.

Why do roller coasters stop on the lift hill?

The control system stops trains at safe points if a sensor reports anything unexpected. The anti-rollback ratchet holds the train on the lift.
