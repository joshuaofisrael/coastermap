What is airtime on a roller coaster?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: airtime is the floating moment when you lift out of your seat, held in by your restraint. It happens when the track falls away faster than you would, so the forces on you drop below zero g. Floater airtime is gentle; ejector airtime is sharp and strong.

Airtime, defined

Airtime is the moment on a roller coaster when you lift up out of your seat, held in only by your restraint. It happens when the track drops away faster than you would fall freely, so the forces on you dip below zero g and you are briefly pulled upwards relative to the seat. Fans split it into two kinds.

  • Floater airtime: a gentle, sustained float close to zero g, usually over a large rounded hill. You hover just above the seat for a second or two.
  • Ejector airtime: a sharper, stronger negative g, usually over a pointed hill or a sudden drop, that pushes you firmly up into the lap bar.

Where you find airtime

Airtime comes from the shape of the track rather than height alone. Hypercoasters are built around a series of tall "camelback" hills for floater airtime, and hybrid and modern wooden coasters use steep, quick hills and wave turns for ejector airtime. An airtime hill is any hill designed for that moment, and a "stall" is an inversion held slowly at the top so riders hang in the restraint.

Is airtime safe?

Airtime is a designed part of the ride. Restraints are built to hold riders through negative g, and parks set height and body-size requirements so the restraint fits. Follow the instructions of ride staff, keep your restraint as it was checked, and do not ride if a park's warning sign covers a condition you have.

Seat choice

The back rows usually get the strongest airtime on the first drop, because the train pulls the back car over the crest at its highest speed. On later hills the front can be stronger, depending on the layout. Rides with lap bars rather than shoulder harnesses make airtime feel more dramatic.

Frequently asked questions

What is airtime on a roller coaster?

Airtime is the moment you lift out of your seat because the forces drop below zero g, usually over a hill or at the top of a drop. Your restraint holds you in.

What is ejector airtime?

A strong, sudden form of airtime that pushes you firmly up into your restraint, usually over a pointed hill.

What is floater airtime?

A gentle, sustained floating feeling close to zero g, usually over a large rounded hill.

Where do you get the most airtime on a roller coaster?

Usually in the back rows on the first drop, and on rides with lap bars rather than shoulder harnesses.
