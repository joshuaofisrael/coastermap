Why your stomach drops on a roller coaster

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: on a drop the seat briefly stops supporting your full weight, so you feel lighter than normal (below 1 g). Your organs shift slightly and your inner ear senses the change, and your brain reads it as the stomach drop. Adrenaline does the rest.

What you are feeling

When a coaster crests a hill and starts down the other side, the train begins to fall faster than your body expects. For a moment the seat is no longer pushing up on you as hard as gravity pulls you down. Your organs are not held rigidly in place, so they shift slightly, and the nerves that sense that movement send a signal your brain reads as the "stomach drop" or "butterflies". At the same moment, the balance organs in your inner ear detect the change in vertical acceleration, and your body releases adrenaline, which adds the tingling, heart-racing feeling.

G-forces in plain English

Engineers describe the forces on a rider in g. Sitting still, you feel 1 g: the normal pull of gravity. At the bottom of a valley or through a tight loop you are pressed into the seat with more than 1 g, so you feel heavier. Over the top of a hill or at the start of a drop you can feel less than 1 g, so you feel lighter. At 0 g you feel weightless, and below 0 g (negative g) you are lifted out of the seat against your lap bar or harness. Coaster fans call those floating moments airtime.

Why some drops feel stronger than others

  • Shape of the hill. A sharply pointed hill changes direction quickly and produces a sudden, strong float. A rounded hill gives a gentler floating feeling.
  • Seat position. In the back of the train you are pulled over the crest at speed, so the drop starts more suddenly. See best seat on a roller coaster.
  • Restraint. A lap bar leaves your upper body free, so floating feels stronger than with an over-the-shoulder harness.
  • Anticipation. Tensing up makes the sensation sharper. Breathing out on the drop helps many riders.

How to make the feeling milder

Sit in the middle or front of the train, keep your back against the seat, look ahead rather than down and breathe out as the train goes over the top. Coasters with rounded hills and no steep drops, such as many family coasters and mine trains, produce only a gentle version of the sensation.

Frequently asked questions

Why does your stomach drop on a roller coaster?

On a drop the seat briefly stops pushing up on you as hard as gravity pulls down, so your organs shift slightly and your inner ear senses the change in vertical acceleration. Your brain reads that as the stomach drop, and adrenaline adds the tingling.

Why do roller coasters make you feel weightless?

Over the top of a hill or at the start of a drop, the train falls away beneath you, so the forces on you drop towards zero g and you feel weightless.

Which seat has the strongest stomach drop?

Usually the back of the train, because it is pulled over the crest at speed.

How can I make the stomach drop feeling less intense?

Sit in the middle or front, keep your back against the seat, look ahead and breathe out on the drop.
