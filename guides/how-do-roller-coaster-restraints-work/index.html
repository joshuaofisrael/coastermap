How do roller coaster restraints work?

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: a lap bar, shoulder harness or vest lowers onto you and locks with a ratchet or hydraulic cylinder, so it can move towards you but not away. Ride staff check restraints before the train leaves, and they stay locked until the station releases them.

Types of restraint

  • Lap bar: a padded bar that lowers across your thighs or hips. Common on family coasters, wooden coasters and hypercoasters, where it leaves your upper body free for airtime.
  • T-bar or individual lap bar: a lap bar shaped to fit between the legs, or one bar per seat, so each rider is held separately.
  • Over-the-shoulder restraint (OTSR): a padded harness that comes down over your shoulders and locks at your waist. Common on looping and inverted coasters.
  • Vest restraint: a softer harness with flexible straps, used on many newer coasters for comfort.
  • Seat belts: many rides add a seat belt as a secondary check, which ride staff confirm before dispatch.

How they lock

Most restraints use a ratchet or hydraulic cylinder that lets the bar move towards you but not away. Once it is locked, it stays locked until the station system releases it at the end of the ride. Ride staff check restraints before the train leaves.

Fit, body size and height

Restraints are one reason parks set height requirements: a rider has to be tall enough for the bar or harness to hold them correctly. Body shape matters too. Universal Orlando's official rider guide says: "Guests who do not fit properly within any safety restraint will not be permitted to ride," and encourages larger guests to try the test seats at the entrance of certain rides. If you are unsure, try the test seat before you queue. See can you be too tall for a roller coaster?

Tips

  • Sit all the way back before the restraint comes down, so it locks in the right place.
  • Empty your pockets; loose items can stop a bar closing fully and can fall out.
  • Follow the ride's instructions about bags, phones and glasses.

Examples

Inverted coasters, where your legs hang free, commonly use shoulder harnesses. Examples we cover:

  • Raptor (Cedar Point): 54 in (137 cm) minimum
  • Batman The Ride (Six Flags Magic Mountain): 54 in (137 cm) minimum
  • Montu (Busch Gardens Tampa Bay): 54 in (137 cm) minimum
  • Phoenix Rising (Busch Gardens Tampa Bay): 42 in (107 cm) minimum
  • Invertigo (Kings Island): 54 in (137 cm) minimum
  • Banshee (Kings Island): 52 in (132 cm) minimum

Frequently asked questions

How do roller coaster restraints lock?

Most use a ratchet or hydraulic cylinder that lets the bar move towards you but not away, locked until the station releases it. Ride staff check restraints before dispatch.

What is an OTSR on a roller coaster?

An over-the-shoulder restraint: a padded harness that comes down over your shoulders and locks at your waist.

What if the restraint does not fit me?

Parks will not let a rider ride if the restraint does not fit properly. Try the test seat at the ride entrance where one is provided.
