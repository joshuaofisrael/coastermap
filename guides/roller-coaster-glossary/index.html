Roller coaster glossary

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Quick answer: this glossary explains 53 roller coaster terms in plain English, from airtime and block brakes to inversions, launches and the zero-g roll, with links to our detailed explainers.

Jump to: A · B · C · D · E · F · G · H · I · L · M · O · P · R · S · T · U · V · W · Z

A

Airtime
The moment you lift out of your seat, held in by your restraint, when forces drop below zero g. Read more.
Anti-rollback
The ratchet on a lift hill that stops a train rolling backwards; it makes the clacking sound. Read more.

B

B&M
Bolliger & Mabillard, a Swiss coaster manufacturer. Read more.
Banked turn
A turn tilted towards its centre so riders are pushed into the seat rather than sideways.
Block brake
A brake that divides the track into sections so only one train is in each section at a time. Read more.
Boomerang
A Vekoma shuttle coaster model that runs its looping course forwards, then backwards. Read more.
Brake run
The section of brakes that slows the train before the station. Read more.

C

Camelback
A large, rounded hill designed for floater airtime.
Catch car
On cable and hydraulic launches, the car that hooks the train, pulls it to speed and lets go. Read more.
Chain lift
A moving chain under the track that pulls the train up the lift hill. Read more.
Cobra roll
A two-inversion element shaped like a cobra's hood that reverses the train's direction.
Corkscrew
An inversion where the track twists through 360 degrees as it moves forward.

D

Dive coaster
A coaster with wide trains that pause at the top of a vertical drop. Read more.
Dive loop
An inversion that rises, twists and dives down, the reverse of an immelmann.

E

Ejector airtime
Strong, sharp airtime that pushes you up into your restraint. Read more.

F

Family coaster
A coaster built for children and adults to ride together. Read more.
Floater airtime
Gentle, sustained airtime close to zero g. Read more.
Flying coaster
A coaster where riders lie face down, parallel to the track. Read more.

G

G-force
The force on a rider compared with normal gravity; 1 g is sitting still. Read more.
Giga coaster
A coaster with a height or drop of 300 to 399 feet. Read more.

H

Heartline roll
A roll where the track rotates around the rider's heart line, so the body stays centred.
Helix
A long, spiralling turn of 360 degrees or more, often with sustained g-forces.
Hybrid coaster
A coaster with steel track on a wooden-style structure, or a wooden coaster retracked in steel. Read more.
Hypercoaster
A coaster with a height or drop of at least 200 feet. Read more.

I

Immelmann
An inversion with a half loop up followed by a half twist, named after a flying manoeuvre.
Inversion
Any element that turns riders upside down. Read more.
Inverted coaster
A coaster where the train hangs below the track and riders' feet dangle. Read more.

L

Lap bar
A restraint that lowers across the thighs or hips. Read more.
Launch
Acceleration that replaces a lift hill, using magnets, hydraulics, air or tyres. Read more.
Lift hill
The climb at the start of most coasters, using a chain, cable or tyres. Read more.
LIM and LSM
Linear induction and linear synchronous motors, electromagnetic launch systems. Read more.

M

Magnetic brakes
Brakes that slow the train with eddy currents, without contact. Read more.
Mine train
A family coaster themed to a runaway mining train, usually with no inversions.

O

OTSR
Over-the-shoulder restraint: a harness that comes down over the shoulders. Read more.
Outlaw hill
A small, sudden hill designed for a quick moment of ejector airtime.
Overbanked turn
A turn banked beyond 90 degrees, so riders tilt partly upside down without fully inverting.

P

Pretzel loop
A loop on a flying coaster where riders dive down on their backs and pull up face down, with strong g-forces. Read more.

R

Rider swap
A system at many parks where adults take turns riding while one waits with a child, without queuing twice. Also called child swap or rider switch. Read more.
Rollback
When a launched train does not clear a tall tower and rolls back to the launch track. Read more.

S

Shuttle coaster
A coaster that runs its course forwards and then backwards rather than completing a circuit. Read more.
Side friction wheels
Wheels on the side of the rail that stop the train moving sideways. Read more.
SLC
Suspended Looping Coaster, a Vekoma inverted coaster model. Read more.
Spinning coaster
A coaster whose cars rotate freely as they travel. Read more.
Stall
An inversion held slowly at the top so riders hang in their restraints.
Strata coaster
A coaster with a height or drop of at least 400 feet. Read more.

T

Top hat
A tall tower element, usually on a launch coaster, where the track climbs vertically and drops straight back down.
Trim brake
A brake partway through a course that takes off a little speed. Read more.

U

Upstop wheels
Wheels under the rail that stop the train lifting off the track. Read more.

V

Vertical loop
A full loop where the train turns upside down; modern loops are teardrop shaped. Read more.

W

Wild mouse
A compact coaster with single cars and sharp, unbanked hairpin turns. Read more.
Wing coaster
A coaster with seats on either side of the track and nothing above or below. Read more.
Wooden coaster
A coaster with track made of laminated wood. Read more.

Z

Zero-g roll
A twisting inversion over a hill where riders feel close to weightless while upside down.

Frequently asked questions

What is airtime on a roller coaster?

The moment you lift out of your seat, held in by your restraint, when forces drop below zero g.

What is an inversion on a roller coaster?

Any element that turns riders upside down, such as a loop, corkscrew or zero-g roll.

What is a giga coaster?

A roller coaster with a height or drop of 300 to 399 feet. Coasters of 200 ft or more are hypercoasters and 400 ft or more strata coasters.
