Pantheon

Busch Gardens Williamsburg · Williamsburg, Virginia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 52 in (132 cm); top speed 73 mph; 178 ft tall; 2 inversions; opened 2022.

Pantheon is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Busch Gardens Williamsburg in Williamsburg, Virginia, built by Intamin Amusement Rides. It opened on 25 Mar 2022. The ride stands 178 ft tall, reaches 73 mph and runs 3,328 ft of track. Instead of a slow chain lift, trains are launched up to speed. It turns riders upside down 2 times. Its steepest drop is 95°, past vertical.

What riding Pantheon is like

Pantheon pairs a 178 ft structure with 3,328 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. The steepest drop is 95°, past vertical, so riders tip slightly beyond straight down. It goes upside down 2 times across 3,328 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2022, it is the 9th oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Busch Gardens Williamsburg.

A rider who just meets its 52 in minimum can also ride 7 other coasters at Busch Gardens Williamsburg, out of 10 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Pantheon sits on the east side of Busch Gardens Williamsburg's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Tempesto, roughly 120 m away (54 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Apollo's Chariot at about 280 m. Pantheon layout, trains and ride system

Pantheon starts with a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that starts while the train is already moving rather than relying only on gravity from a lift. RCDB also lists 1 booster section (LSM Boost (multi-pass)) that add speed mid-course.

These are the 7 elements RCDB records on Pantheon's track, in RCDB's order:

  1. LSM Launch (rolling)
  2. Zero-G Winder
  3. LSM Boost (multi-pass)
  4. Vertical Rollback
  5. Top Hat - Outside
  6. Outward Banked Airtime Hill
  7. Zero-G Stall

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 2 times. Riders must be at least 52 in (132 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

Before you ride Pantheon

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 52 in (132 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 2 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Busch Gardens Williamsburg app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Pantheon?

Riders must be at least 52 in (132 cm) tall to ride Pantheon, according to Busch Gardens Williamsburg, Roller Coasters page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Pantheon?

Pantheon is 178 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Pantheon go?

Its top speed is 73 mph (117 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Pantheon go upside down?

Yes. It has 2 inversions.

Is Pantheon scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Pantheon open?

Pantheon opened on 25 Mar 2022.

Who built Pantheon?

Pantheon was manufactured by Intamin Amusement Rides.

How long is the track on Pantheon?

RCDB lists 3,328 ft (1,014 m) of track.

Does Pantheon launch?

Yes. RCDB lists a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that starts while the train is already moving.

How many people ride Pantheon at once?

Up to 20 per train, going by RCDB's train layout.

Is Pantheon good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 2 times; it tops out at 73 mph; the minimum height is 52 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
