Grover's Alpine Express

Busch Gardens Williamsburg · Williamsburg, Virginia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 41 in (104 cm), or 38 to 41 in (97 to 104 cm) with a companion; top speed 24.9 mph; 24 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2009.

Grover's Alpine Express is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Busch Gardens Williamsburg in Williamsburg, Virginia, built by Zierer. It opened on 3 Apr 2009. The ride stands 24 ft tall, reaches 24.9 mph and runs 629.9 ft of track. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Grover's Alpine Express is like

At 24 ft tall with 629.9 ft of track, Grover's Alpine Express balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns.

Opened in 2009, it is the 5th oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Busch Gardens Williamsburg. It is one of 5 rides on Coaster Map built to Zierer's Force / 190 design, and the first of them to open. Others include Air Grover at Busch Gardens Tampa, Fun Pilot at Walibi Belgium, Cowabunga Carts at Mirabilandia and Godiståget at Kolmården. Height rules for this model differ by park: Busch Gardens Williamsburg sets 41 in, while Busch Gardens Tampa sets 38 in, Walibi Belgium sets 120 cm and Mirabilandia sets 90 cm.

Its 41 in minimum is the lowest of any coaster at Busch Gardens Williamsburg, which makes it a natural first coaster there. Among family-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 47 in (120 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class. Children from 38 to 41 in (97 to 104 cm) can ride with a companion, so a family can often ride together before a child is old enough to ride alone.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Grover's Alpine Express sits on the north side of Busch Gardens Williamsburg's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Loch Ness Monster, roughly 250 m away (48 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Tempesto at about 260 m. Grover's Alpine Express layout, trains and ride system

Trains climb a lift hill driven by rubber booster wheels. RCDB also lists 1 booster section (Booster Wheel Lift Hill) that add speed mid-course.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Grover's Alpine Express?

Riders must be at least 41 in (104 cm) tall to ride Grover's Alpine Express, or 38 to 41 in (97 to 104 cm) with a companion, according to Busch Gardens Williamsburg, Grover's Alpine Express ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Grover's Alpine Express?

Grover's Alpine Express is 24 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Grover's Alpine Express go?

Its top speed is 24.9 mph (40 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Grover's Alpine Express go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Grover's Alpine Express scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a family coaster. It is built for families.

When did Grover's Alpine Express open?

Grover's Alpine Express opened on 3 Apr 2009.

Who built Grover's Alpine Express?

Grover's Alpine Express was manufactured by Zierer.

How long is the track on Grover's Alpine Express?

RCDB lists 629.9 ft of track.

How does Grover's Alpine Express get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a lift hill driven by rubber booster wheels; it has no launch.

How many people ride Grover's Alpine Express at once?

Each train carries 16 riders, according to RCDB.

Is Grover's Alpine Express good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as family; it has no inversions; it tops out at 24.9 mph; the minimum height is 41 in. Those are the marks of a first-coaster ride: a gentle class and no inversions.

Is Grover's Alpine Express at Busch Gardens Williamsburg the same as Air Grover at Busch Gardens Tampa?

Both are Zierer Force / 190 coasters on RCDB; the Busch Gardens Williamsburg ride opened in 2009 and the Busch Gardens Tampa ride in 2010; Busch Gardens Williamsburg sets 41 in and Busch Gardens Tampa sets 38 in.
