Alpengeist

Busch Gardens Williamsburg · Williamsburg, Virginia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm); top speed 67 mph; 195 ft tall; 6 inversions; opened 1997.

Alpengeist is a steel inverted roller coaster at Busch Gardens Williamsburg in Williamsburg, Virginia, built by Bolliger & Mabillard. It opened on 22 Mar 1997. The ride stands 195 ft tall, drops 170 ft, reaches 67 mph and runs 3,828 ft of track. On an inverted coaster the seats hang below the track, so riders' feet dangle free with nothing beneath them. It turns riders upside down 6 times.

What riding Alpengeist is like

Alpengeist pairs a 195 ft structure with 3,828 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. It goes upside down 6 times across 3,828 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements. RCDB lists a peak force of 3.7 g, below the 4 g median for coasters on Coaster Map with a g-force figure.

Opened in 1997, it is the 2nd oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Busch Gardens Williamsburg.

Its 54 in minimum is the strictest at Busch Gardens Williamsburg, shared with 2 other coasters. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Alpengeist sits on the west side of Busch Gardens Williamsburg's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Griffon, roughly 100 m away (54 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is InvadR at about 140 m. Alpengeist layout, trains and ride system

To gain height it uses a chain lift hill.

RCDB lists 8 track elements for Alpengeist, shown here in the order RCDB lists them:

  1. Chain Lift Hill
  2. Immelmann
  3. Loop (106 ft tall)
  4. Tunnel
  5. Cobra Roll
  6. Block Brake
  7. Zero-G Roll
  8. Corkscrew

RCDB counts 6 inversions in total; some elements, such as a cobra roll, turn riders over more than once.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 6 times. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

On this style of coaster, the front row has nothing in your line of sight, and the outermost seats feel the most exposed. Riders after the strongest drops pick the back row, which is pulled over each crest faster.

Before you ride Alpengeist

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 54 in (137 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 6 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Busch Gardens Williamsburg app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Alpengeist?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride Alpengeist, according to Busch Gardens Williamsburg, Roller Coasters page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Alpengeist?

Alpengeist is 195 ft tall, with a drop of 170 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Alpengeist go?

Its top speed is 67 mph (108 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Alpengeist go upside down?

Yes. It has 6 inversions.

Is Alpengeist scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Alpengeist open?

Alpengeist opened on 22 Mar 1997.

Who built Alpengeist?

Alpengeist was manufactured by Bolliger & Mabillard.

How long is the ride on Alpengeist?

RCDB lists a ride time of 3 minutes 10 seconds over 3,828 ft of track.

How does Alpengeist get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Alpengeist at once?

RCDB's train details give 32 seats per train. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,820 riders.

Is Alpengeist good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 6 times; it tops out at 67 mph; the minimum height is 54 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
