Loch Ness Monster

Busch Gardens Williamsburg · Williamsburg, Virginia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 48 in (122 cm); top speed 60 mph; 130 ft tall; 2 inversions; opened 1978.

Loch Ness Monster is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Busch Gardens Williamsburg in Williamsburg, Virginia, built by Arrow Dynamics. It opened in 1978. The ride stands 130 ft tall, drops 114.2 ft, reaches 60 mph and runs 3,240 ft of track. The layout follows the natural hillside terrain. It turns riders upside down 2 times.

What riding Loch Ness Monster is like

At 130 ft tall with 3,240 ft of track, Loch Ness Monster balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns. It goes upside down 2 times across 3,240 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements. RCDB lists a peak force of 3.5 g, below the 4 g median for coasters on Coaster Map with a g-force figure.

Opened in 1978, it is the oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Busch Gardens Williamsburg.

A rider who just meets its 48 in minimum can also ride 5 other coasters at Busch Gardens Williamsburg, out of 10 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Loch Ness Monster sits on the north-west side of Busch Gardens Williamsburg's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Alpengeist, roughly 150 m away (54 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Griffon at about 150 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 2 times. Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

How Loch Ness Monster compares

On our intensity ranking Loch Ness Monster is number 6 of 11 at Busch Gardens Williamsburg, where 1 is the most intense. It is the 6th tallest of the park's 7 coasters with a listed height.

Before you ride Loch Ness Monster

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 48 in (122 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 2 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Busch Gardens Williamsburg app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Loch Ness Monster?

Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall to ride Loch Ness Monster, according to Busch Gardens Williamsburg, Roller Coasters page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Loch Ness Monster?

Loch Ness Monster is 130 ft tall, with a drop of 114.2 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Loch Ness Monster go?

Its top speed is 60 mph (97 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Loch Ness Monster go upside down?

Yes. It has 2 inversions.

Is Loch Ness Monster scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Loch Ness Monster open?

Loch Ness Monster opened in 1978.

Who built Loch Ness Monster?

Loch Ness Monster was manufactured by Arrow Dynamics.

How long is the ride on Loch Ness Monster?

RCDB lists a ride time of 2 minutes 10 seconds over 3,240 ft of track.

How does Loch Ness Monster get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Loch Ness Monster at once?

RCDB's train details give 28 seats per train.

Is Loch Ness Monster good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 2 times; it tops out at 60 mph; the minimum height is 48 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
