The Big One

Blackpool Pleasure Beach · Blackpool, England

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 132 cm (about 52 in); top speed 74 mph; 213 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 1994.

The Big One is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Blackpool Pleasure Beach in Blackpool, England, built by Arrow Dynamics. It first opened on 28 May 1994 and was previously known as Pepsi Max Big One. The ride stands 213 ft tall, drops 205 ft, reaches 74 mph and runs 5,497 ft of track. That height makes it a hyper coaster (200 to 299 ft tall). It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding The Big One is like

The Big One pairs a 213 ft structure with 5,497 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. RCDB lists a peak force of 3.5 g, below the 4 g median for coasters on Coaster Map with a g-force figure.

Opened in 1994, it is the 7th oldest of the 9 coasters we list at Blackpool Pleasure Beach. It has run under other names (Pepsi Max Big One), so older guides and photos may use those. It is one of 2 rides on Coaster Map built to Arrow Dynamics's Hyper Coaster design, and the most recent of them to open. Others include Magnum XL-200 at Cedar Point. Height rules for this model differ by park: Blackpool Pleasure Beach sets 132 cm, while Cedar Point sets 48 in.

A rider who just meets its 132 cm minimum can also ride 6 other coasters at Blackpool Pleasure Beach, out of 7 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, The Big One sits on the south side of Blackpool Pleasure Beach's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Steeplechase, roughly 60 m away (127 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Revolution at about 70 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 132 cm (about 52 in) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for The Big One?

Riders must be at least 132 cm (about 52 in) tall to ride The Big One, according to Pleasure Beach Resort, Height Restrictions page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is The Big One?

The Big One is 213 ft tall, with a drop of 205 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does The Big One go?

Its top speed is 74 mph (119 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does The Big One go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is The Big One scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did The Big One open?

The Big One opened on 28 May 1994 (previously known as Pepsi Max Big One).

Who built The Big One?

The Big One was manufactured by Arrow Dynamics.

How long is the ride on The Big One?

RCDB lists a ride time of 3 minutes over 5,497 ft of track.

How does The Big One get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride The Big One at once?

RCDB's train details give 30 seats per train. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,700 riders.

Is The Big One good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it has no inversions; it tops out at 74 mph; the minimum height is 132 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is The Big One at Blackpool Pleasure Beach the same as Magnum XL-200 at Cedar Point?

Both are Arrow Dynamics Hyper Coaster coasters on RCDB; the Blackpool Pleasure Beach ride opened in 1994 and the Cedar Point ride in 1989; Blackpool Pleasure Beach sets 132 cm and Cedar Point sets 48 in; RCDB lists heights of 213 ft and 205 ft.
