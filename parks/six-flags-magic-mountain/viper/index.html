Viper

Six Flags Magic Mountain · Valencia, California

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm); top speed 70 mph; 188 ft tall; 7 inversions; opened 1990.

Viper is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California, built by Arrow Dynamics. It opened on 7 Apr 1990. The ride stands 188 ft tall, drops 171 ft, reaches 70 mph and runs 3,830 ft of track. It turns riders upside down 7 times.

What riding Viper is like

At 188 ft tall with 3,830 ft of track, Viper balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns. It goes upside down 7 times across 3,830 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 1990, it is the 4th oldest of the 16 coasters we list at Six Flags Magic Mountain.

Its 54 in minimum is the strictest at Magic Mountain, shared with 6 other coasters. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Viper sits on the south-east side of Magic Mountain's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Great American Revolution, roughly 250 m away, so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Tatsu at about 330 m. Viper layout, trains and ride system

To gain height it uses a chain lift hill.

RCDB lists 6 track elements for Viper, shown here in the order RCDB lists them:

  1. Chain Lift Hill
  2. Loop (144 ft tall)
  3. Loop (90 ft tall)
  4. Loop (62 ft tall)
  5. Batwing
  6. Double Corkscrew (40 ft tall)

RCDB counts 7 inversions in total; some elements, such as a cobra roll, turn riders over more than once.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Magic Mountain rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 7 times. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Before you ride Viper

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 54 in (137 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 7 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Six Flags Magic Mountain app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Viper?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride Viper, according to Six Flags Magic Mountain, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Viper?

Viper is 188 ft tall, with a drop of 171 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Viper go?

Its top speed is 70 mph (113 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Viper go upside down?

Yes. It has 7 inversions.

Is Viper scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Magic Mountain rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Viper open?

Viper opened on 7 Apr 1990.

Who built Viper?

Viper was manufactured by Arrow Dynamics.

How long is the ride on Viper?

RCDB lists a ride time of 2 minutes 30 seconds over 3,830 ft of track.

How does Viper get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Viper at once?

Up to 28 per train, going by RCDB's train layout. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,700 riders.

Is Viper good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 7 times; it tops out at 70 mph; the minimum height is 54 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Viper at Magic Mountain the same as Viper at Six Flags Darien Lake?

Both are Arrow Dynamics Custom Looping Coaster coasters on RCDB; the Magic Mountain ride opened in 1990 and the Six Flags Darien Lake ride in 1982; Magic Mountain sets 54 in and Six Flags Darien Lake sets 48 in; RCDB lists heights of 188 ft and 121 ft.
