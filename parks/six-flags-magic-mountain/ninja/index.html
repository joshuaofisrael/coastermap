Ninja

Six Flags Magic Mountain · Valencia, California

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 42 in (107 cm); top speed 55 mph; 60 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 1988.

Ninja is a steel suspended roller coaster at Six Flags Magic Mountain in Valencia, California, built by Arrow Dynamics. It opened on 21 May 1988. The ride stands 60 ft tall, reaches 55 mph and runs 2,700 ft of track. On a suspended coaster the cars hang below the track on pivots and swing outward through the turns. The layout follows the natural hillside terrain. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Ninja is like

Ninja spreads 2,700 ft of track across a layout only 60 ft tall, so expect a ride that keeps moving through turns and hills instead of saving everything for one drop.

Opened in 1988, it is the 3rd oldest of the 16 coasters we list at Six Flags Magic Mountain. It is one of 5 rides on Coaster Map built to Arrow Dynamics's Suspended Coaster design, and the 2nd of them to open. Others include Iron Dragon at Cedar Point, The Bat at Kings Island, Vortex at Canada's Wonderland and Vampire at Chessington. Height rules for this model differ by park: Magic Mountain sets 42 in, while Chessington sets 130 cm.

A rider who just meets its 42 in minimum can also ride 1 other coaster at Magic Mountain, out of 13 with verified rules. Among thrill-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 48 in (122 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Ninja sits on the south side of Magic Mountain's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is West Coast Racers, roughly 130 m away (54 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Tatsu at about 130 m. How intense is it?

Moderate (thrill)

RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster; Six Flags Magic Mountain rates it 2 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits riders who enjoy speed and turns but may not want the biggest drops in the park and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 42 in (107 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Ninja?

Riders must be at least 42 in (107 cm) tall to ride Ninja, according to Six Flags Magic Mountain, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Ninja?

Ninja is 60 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Ninja go?

Its top speed is 55 mph (88 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Ninja go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Ninja scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster; Six Flags Magic Mountain rates it 2 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. Expect a lively ride that stops short of the most extreme coasters.

When did Ninja open?

Ninja opened on 21 May 1988.

Who built Ninja?

Ninja was manufactured by Arrow Dynamics.

How long is the ride on Ninja?

RCDB lists a ride time of 1 minute 30 seconds over 2,700 ft of track.

How does Ninja get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Ninja at once?

Up to 28 per train, going by RCDB's train layout. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,600 riders.

Is Ninja good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as thrill; it has no inversions; it tops out at 55 mph; the minimum height is 42 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Ninja at Magic Mountain the same as Iron Dragon at Cedar Point?

Both are Arrow Dynamics Suspended Coaster coasters on RCDB; the Magic Mountain ride opened in 1988 and the Cedar Point ride in 1987; both list a 42 in minimum; RCDB lists heights of 60 ft and 76 ft.
