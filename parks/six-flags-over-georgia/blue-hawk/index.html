Blue Hawk

Six Flags Over Georgia · Austell, Georgia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 48 in (122 cm); top speed 52 mph; 122 ft tall; 5 inversions; opened 1992.

Blue Hawk is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Six Flags Over Georgia in Austell, Georgia, built by Vekoma. It first opened on 1 Mar 1992 and was previously known as Ninja. The ride stands 122 ft tall, reaches 52 mph and runs 2739.5 ft of track. It turns riders upside down 5 times.

What riding Blue Hawk is like

Blue Hawk pairs a 122 ft structure with 2,739.5 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. It goes upside down 5 times across 2,739.5 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 1992, it is the 4th oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Six Flags Over Georgia. It has run under other names (Ninja), so older guides and photos may use those.

A rider who just meets its 48 in minimum can also ride 6 other coasters at Six Flags Over Georgia, out of 10 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Blue Hawk sits on the south side of Six Flags Over Georgia's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Great American Scream Machine, roughly 180 m away (48 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Superman - Ultimate Flight at about 240 m. Blue Hawk layout, trains and ride system

To gain height it uses a chain lift hill.

These are the 4 elements RCDB records on Blue Hawk's track, in RCDB's order:

  1. Chain Lift Hill
  2. Butterfly
  3. Reverse Sidewinder
  4. Double Corkscrew

RCDB counts 5 inversions in total; some elements, such as a cobra roll, turn riders over more than once.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Over Georgia rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 5 times. Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

Before you ride Blue Hawk

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 48 in (122 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 5 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Six Flags Over Georgia app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Blue Hawk?

Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall to ride Blue Hawk, according to Six Flags Over Georgia, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Blue Hawk?

Blue Hawk is 122 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Blue Hawk go?

Its top speed is 52 mph (84 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Blue Hawk go upside down?

Yes. It has 5 inversions.

Is Blue Hawk scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Over Georgia rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Blue Hawk open?

Blue Hawk opened on 1 Mar 1992 (previously known as Ninja).

Who built Blue Hawk?

Blue Hawk was manufactured by Vekoma.

How long is the ride on Blue Hawk?

RCDB lists a ride time of 1 minute 20 seconds over 2739.5 ft of track.

How does Blue Hawk get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Blue Hawk at once?

RCDB's train details give 28 seats per train.

What restraints does Blue Hawk use?

RCDB lists: shoulder harness.

Is Blue Hawk good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 5 times; it tops out at 52 mph; the minimum height is 48 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Where did Blue Hawk operate before?

RCDB records it at Dinosaur Beach (as Kamikaze).
