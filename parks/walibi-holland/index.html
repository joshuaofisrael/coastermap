Walibi Holland height requirements and roller coasters ranked

Biddinghuizen, Netherlands · 9 coasters

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Walibi Holland in Biddinghuizen, Netherlands has 9 operating roller coasters on RCDB. Height requirements run from 105 cm (about 41.3 in) on Eat My Dust to 140 cm (about 55.1 in) on Condor. Riding alone, a 42-inch (107 cm) child can ride 1, a 48-inch (122 cm) child 6 and a 54-inch (137 cm) rider 6 of them.

What the coasters at Walibi Holland are like

RCDB classes 2 coasters here as kiddie or family, 0 as thrill and 7 as extreme, which makes Walibi Holland a thrill-heavy park for roller coasters. Extreme coasters make up 78% of its lineup, against 42% across all 189 parks on Coaster Map.

The typical (median) height requirement here is about 47.2 in, lower than the 48 in typical across the site, and 3 of the 9 coasters with a verified rule need a rider of at least 48 in (122 cm).

Of the ride types, 6 of the 9 go upside down.

The most common builder is Vekoma, with 3 of the coasters.

The longest-serving coaster in the lineup is Drako, which opened in 1992.

Walibi Holland publishes its height rules in centimetres. We show the official centimetre figure with an inch conversion, and every list on this page compares in centimetres, so a child exactly at the line counts as tall enough.

What can my child ride at Walibi Holland? By height

At Walibi Holland the biggest single step is reaching 48 in (122 cm), which opens up 5 more coasters than the height below. Measure at home in the shoes your child will wear, since parks measure at the ride entrance and a child even slightly under the line is turned away.

36 in (91 cm) tall

A 36-inch rider is below the ride-alone minimum on every coaster here. With a companion they can also ride Drako (90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in)) and Eat My Dust (90 to 105 cm (about 35.4 to 41.3 in)). Still too short for 7 coasters, the nearest being Xpress: Platform 13 at 120 cm (about 47.2 in).

42 in (107 cm) tall

A 42-inch rider meets the ride-alone minimum on 1 of 9 coasters with a verified rule: Eat My Dust. With a companion they can also ride Drako (90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in)). Still too short for 7 coasters, the nearest being Xpress: Platform 13 at 120 cm (about 47.2 in).

48 in (122 cm) tall

A 48-inch rider meets the ride-alone minimum on 6 of 9 coasters with a verified rule, 5 more than at the height below: Xpress: Platform 13, Speed of Sound, Drako, Untamed and YoY. Still too short for 3 coasters, the nearest being Condor at 140 cm (about 55.1 in).

54 in (137 cm) tall

A 54-inch rider meets the ride-alone minimum on 6 of 9 coasters with a verified rule: Xpress: Platform 13, Speed of Sound, Drako, Untamed, Eat My Dust and YoY. Still too short for 3 coasters, the nearest being Condor at 140 cm (about 55.1 in).

Companion rules at Walibi Holland

Best coasters at Walibi Holland for first-timers

Nervous riders usually do best working up in steps. Here is a three-step plan for Walibi Holland, picked from our intensity ranking and the official height rules:

  1. Start here: Drako, the gentlest coaster here by our intensity ranking, with no inversions (120 cm minimum; 19.7 ft tall, 19.9 mph, no inversions).
  2. Step up: Eat My Dust, a bigger ride that stops short of the park's most extreme coasters (105 cm minimum; 38.7 ft tall, 24.9 mph, no inversions).
  3. The big one: YoY, the most intense coaster in the park (120 cm minimum; 95.1 ft tall, 51 mph, 6 inversions, 4 g).

Sit in the middle of the train for the softest ride, and watch a few cycles from the queue first. Our best first roller coasters guide has more tips.

Frequently asked questions

What are the height requirements at Walibi Holland?

Roller coaster height requirements at Walibi Holland run from 105 cm (about 41.3 in) (Eat My Dust) to 140 cm (about 55.1 in) (Condor), according to official park sources checked 10 Nov 2026. Rules are checked at each ride entrance.

Walibi Holland has 9 operating roller coasters according to RCDB, checked 10 Nov 2026.

What is the scariest roller coaster at Walibi Holland?
