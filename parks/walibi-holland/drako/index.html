Drako

Walibi Holland · Biddinghuizen, Netherlands

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 120 cm (about 47.2 in), or 90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in) with a companion; top speed 19.9 mph; 19.7 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 1992.

Drako is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Walibi Holland in Biddinghuizen, Netherlands, built by Zierer. It first opened on 15 May 1992 and was previously known as Keverbaan and later Road Runner Express and later Wok's Waanzin and later Rattlesnake. The ride stands 19.7 ft tall, reaches 19.9 mph and runs 652.9 ft of track. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Drako is like

Drako spreads 652.9 ft of track across a layout only 19.7 ft tall, so expect a ride that keeps moving through turns and hills instead of saving everything for one drop.

Opened in 1992, it is the oldest of the 9 coasters we list at Walibi Holland. It has run under other names (Keverbaan, then Road Runner Express, then Wok's Waanzin, then Rattlesnake), so older guides and photos may use those. It is one of 7 rides on Coaster Map built to Zierer's Tivoli / Medium design, and the 4th of them to open. Others include SOS Tournevis at Parc Astérix, Ladybird at Lightwater Valley, Children Coaster at Nagashima Spa Land and Alap Alap at Dufan. Height rules for this model differ by park: Walibi Holland sets 120 cm, while Lightwater Valley sets 90 cm, Dufan sets 100 cm and Adventureland Long Island sets 48 in.

A rider who just meets its 120 cm minimum can also ride 5 other coasters at Walibi Holland, out of 8 with verified rules. Among family-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 47 in (120 cm), so this rule is about typical for its class. Children from 90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in) can ride with a companion, so a family can often ride together before a child is old enough to ride alone.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Drako sits on the north-east side of Walibi Holland's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Lost Gravity, roughly 110 m away (140 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Speed of Sound at about 270 m. Drako layout, trains and ride system

Trains climb a lift hill driven by rubber booster wheels. RCDB also lists 1 booster section (Booster Wheel Lift Hill) that add speed mid-course.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Drako?

Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall to ride Drako, or 90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in) with a companion, according to Walibi Holland, official attraction page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Drako?

Drako is 19.7 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Drako go?

Its top speed is 19.9 mph (32 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Drako go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Drako scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a family coaster. It is built for families.

When did Drako open?

Drako opened on 15 May 1992 (previously known as Keverbaan and later Road Runner Express and later Wok's Waanzin and later Rattlesnake).

Who built Drako?

Drako was manufactured by Zierer.

How long is the track on Drako?

RCDB lists 652.9 ft of track.

How does Drako get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a lift hill driven by rubber booster wheels; it has no launch.

How many people ride Drako at once?

Each train carries 26 riders, according to RCDB. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,000 riders.

Is Drako good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as family; it has no inversions; it tops out at 19.9 mph; the minimum height is 120 cm. Those are the marks of a first-coaster ride: a gentle class and no inversions.

Where did Drako operate before?

RCDB records it at Zygo Park (as Tarzungle).

Is Drako at Walibi Holland the same as Children Coaster at Nagashima Spa Land?

Both are Zierer Tivoli / Medium coasters on RCDB; the Walibi Holland ride opened in 1992 and the Nagashima Spa Land ride in 1983.
