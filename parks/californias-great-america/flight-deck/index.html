Flight Deck

California's Great America · Santa Clara, California

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm); top speed 50 mph; 102 ft tall; 3 inversions; opened 1993.

Flight Deck is a steel inverted roller coaster at California's Great America in Santa Clara, California, built by Bolliger & Mabillard. It first opened on 19 Mar 1993 and was previously known as Top Gun. The ride stands 102 ft tall, drops 91 ft, reaches 50 mph and runs 2,260 ft of track. On an inverted coaster the seats hang below the track, so riders' feet dangle free with nothing beneath them. It turns riders upside down 3 times.

What riding Flight Deck is like

At 102 ft tall with 2,260 ft of track, Flight Deck balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns. It goes upside down 3 times across 2,260 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 1993, it is the 5th oldest of the 9 coasters we list at California's Great America. It has run under other names (Top Gun), so older guides and photos may use those.

Its 54 in minimum is the strictest at California's Great America, shared with 1 other coaster. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Flight Deck sits on the north side of California's Great America's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Patriot, roughly 160 m away (54 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Lucy's Crabbie Cabbies at about 340 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; California's Great America rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 3 times. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall, and no taller than 78 in (198 cm).

Best seat to ride

Choose an outside seat for the most open feeling and the front row for a clear view; the back row feels the drops hardest because the train pulls it over the top at speed.

Before you ride Flight Deck

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 54 in (137 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Tall riders: the maximum is 78 in (198 cm). See can you be too tall?
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 3 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Flight Deck?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride Flight Deck. The maximum rider height is 78 in (198 cm), according to California's Great America, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Flight Deck?

Flight Deck is 102 ft tall, with a drop of 91 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Flight Deck go?

Its top speed is 50 mph (80 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Flight Deck go upside down?

Yes. It has 3 inversions.

Is Flight Deck scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; California&#x27;s Great America rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Flight Deck open?

Flight Deck opened on 19 Mar 1993 (previously known as Top Gun).

Who built Flight Deck?

Flight Deck was manufactured by Bolliger & Mabillard.

How long is the ride on Flight Deck?

RCDB lists a ride time of 2 minutes 26 seconds over 2,260 ft of track.

How does Flight Deck get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Flight Deck at once?

RCDB's train details give 28 seats per train. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,380 riders.

Is Flight Deck good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 3 times; it tops out at 50 mph; the minimum height is 54 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
