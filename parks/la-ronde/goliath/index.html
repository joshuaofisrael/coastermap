Goliath

La Ronde · Montréal, Canada

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm); top speed 68.3 mph; 174.8 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2006.

Goliath is a steel sit-down roller coaster at La Ronde in Montréal, Canada, built by Bolliger & Mabillard. It opened on 13 May 2006. The ride stands 174.8 ft tall, drops 170.6 ft, reaches 68.3 mph and runs 4038.8 ft of track. It does not turn riders upside down.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; La Ronde rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Seat advice is general guidance based on how this style of coaster moves, not a park rule.

On our intensity ranking Goliath is number 3 of 8 at La Ronde, where 1 is the most intense. It is the tallest of the park's 6 coasters with a listed height.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Goliath?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride Goliath, according to La Ronde, official All Rides page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Goliath?

Goliath is 174.8 ft tall, with a drop of 170.6 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Goliath go?

Its top speed is 68.3 mph (110 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Goliath go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Goliath scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; La Ronde rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Goliath open?

Goliath opened on 13 May 2006.

Who built Goliath?

Goliath was manufactured by Bolliger & Mabillard.
