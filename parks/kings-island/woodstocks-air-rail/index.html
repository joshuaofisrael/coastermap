Woodstock's Air Rail

Kings Island · Mason, Ohio

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 44 in (112 cm); top speed 26 mph; 48.6 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2001.

Woodstock's Air Rail is a steel inverted roller coaster at Kings Island in Mason, Ohio, built by Vekoma. It first opened on 2001 and was previously known as Flying Ace Aerial Chase and later Rugrats Runaway Reptar. The ride stands 48.6 ft tall, reaches 26 mph and runs 1,122 ft of track. On an inverted coaster the seats hang below the track, so riders' feet dangle free with nothing beneath them. It does not turn riders upside down.

How intense is it?

Mild (family)

RCDB classes it as a family coaster; Kings Island rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits young riders taking their first coasters and adults riding with them and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 44 in (112 cm) tall, and no taller than 76 in (193 cm).

Best seat to ride

The front row gives an unobstructed view, which is the main reason riders choose it on this style of coaster. Outer seats feel more open; the back row is pulled over the drops faster and so tends to give the strongest drop sensation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Woodstock's Air Rail?

Riders must be at least 44 in (112 cm) tall to ride Woodstock's Air Rail. The maximum rider height is 76 in (193 cm), according to Kings Island, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Woodstock's Air Rail?

Woodstock's Air Rail is 48.6 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Woodstock's Air Rail go?

Its top speed is 26 mph (42 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Woodstock's Air Rail go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Woodstock's Air Rail scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a family coaster; Kings Island rates it 4 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. It is built for families.

When did Woodstock's Air Rail open?

Woodstock's Air Rail opened on 2001 (previously known as Flying Ace Aerial Chase and later Rugrats Runaway Reptar).

Who built Woodstock's Air Rail?

Woodstock's Air Rail was manufactured by Vekoma.
