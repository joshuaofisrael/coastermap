Tulireki

Linnanmäki · Helsinki, Finland

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 140 cm (about 55.1 in), or 120 to 140 cm (about 47.2 to 55.1 in) with a companion; top speed 36 mph; 54.2 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2004.

Tulireki is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Linnanmäki in Helsinki, Finland, built by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG. It opened on 29 Apr 2004. The ride stands 54.2 ft tall, reaches 36 mph and runs 1102.3 ft of track. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Tulireki is like

At 54.2 ft tall with 1,102.3 ft of track, Tulireki balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns. RCDB lists a peak force of 3.5 g, below the 4 g median for coasters on Coaster Map with a g-force figure.

Opened in 2004, it is the 4th oldest of the 8 coasters we list at Linnanmäki.

Its 140 cm minimum is the strictest at Linnanmäki, shared with 5 other coasters. Among thrill-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 48 in (122 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class. Children from 120 to 140 cm (about 47.2 to 55.1 in) can ride with a companion, so a family can often ride together before a child is old enough to ride alone.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Tulireki sits on the west side of Linnanmäki's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Pikajuna, roughly 30 m away (130 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Taiga at about 60 m. How intense is it?

Moderate (thrill)

RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits riders who enjoy speed and turns but may not want the biggest drops in the park and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 140 cm (about 55.1 in) tall, or 120 to 140 cm (about 47.2 to 55.1 in) with a companion.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

On our intensity ranking Tulireki is number 6 of 8 at Linnanmäki, where 1 is the most intense. It is the 6th tallest of the park's 8 coasters with a listed height.

Before you ride Tulireki

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 140 cm (about 55.1 in), and riders from 120 to 140 cm (about 47.2 to 55.1 in) need a companion. Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Riding alone requires being over 8; riding with a guardian requires being over 6.
  • Loose items, health warnings and accessibility rules are set by the park and posted at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Linnanmäki app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Tulireki?

Riders must be at least 140 cm (about 55.1 in) tall to ride Tulireki, or 120 to 140 cm (about 47.2 to 55.1 in) with a companion. Riding alone requires being over 8; riding with a guardian requires being over 6, according to Linnanmäki, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Tulireki?

Tulireki is 54.2 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Tulireki go?

Its top speed is 36 mph (58 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Tulireki go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Tulireki scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster. Expect a lively ride that stops short of the most extreme coasters.

When did Tulireki open?

Tulireki opened on 29 Apr 2004.

Who built Tulireki?

Tulireki was manufactured by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG.

How long is the track on Tulireki?

RCDB lists 1102.3 ft of track.

How does Tulireki get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Tulireki at once?

Up to 6 per train, going by RCDB's train layout.

Is Tulireki good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as thrill; it has no inversions; it tops out at 36 mph; the minimum height is 140 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
