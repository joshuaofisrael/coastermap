Takabisha

Fuji-Q Highland · Fujiyoshida, Japan

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 125 cm (about 49.2 in); top speed 62.1 mph; 141 ft tall; 7 inversions; opened 2011.

Takabisha is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Fuji-Q Highland in Fujiyoshida, Japan, built by Gerstlauer Amusement Rides GmbH. It opened on 16 Jul 2011. The ride stands 141 ft tall, reaches 62.1 mph and runs 3280.8 ft of track. It turns riders upside down 7 times. Its steepest drop is 121°, past vertical.

What riding Takabisha is like

At 141 ft tall with 3,280.8 ft of track, Takabisha balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns. The steepest drop is 121°, past vertical, so riders tip slightly beyond straight down. It goes upside down 7 times across 3,280.8 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2011, it is the 5th oldest of the 6 coasters we list at Fuji-Q Highland. It is one of 2 rides on Coaster Map built to Gerstlauer Amusement Rides GmbH's Euro-Fighter / 1000 design, and the first of them to open. Others include TMNT Shellraiser at Nickelodeon Universe NJ. Height rules for this model differ by park: Fuji-Q Highland sets 125 cm, while Nickelodeon Universe NJ sets 48 in.

A rider who just meets its 125 cm minimum can also ride 3 other coasters at Fuji-Q Highland, out of 4 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Takabisha sits on the south side of Fuji-Q Highland's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Nia and Animal Coaster, roughly 220 m away, so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Fujiyama at about 250 m. Takabisha layout, trains and ride system

Takabisha starts with a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that starts while the train is already moving rather than relying only on gravity from a lift. It also uses a vertical chain lift.

Element by element, as listed on RCDB (9 entries):

  1. Heartline Roll
  2. LSM Launch (rolling) (from 0 mph - 62.1 mph in 2 s)
  3. Corkscrew
  4. Banana Roll
  5. Corkscrew
  6. Vertical Chain Lift Hill
  7. Dive Loop
  8. Top Hat - Inside
  9. Immelmann

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 7 times. Riders must be at least 125 cm (about 49.2 in) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Takabisha?

Riders must be at least 125 cm (about 49.2 in) tall to ride Takabisha. Not available to guests over 55, according to Fuji-Q Highland, official attraction page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Takabisha?

Takabisha is 141 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Takabisha go?

Its top speed is 62.1 mph (100 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Takabisha go upside down?

Yes. It has 7 inversions.

Is Takabisha scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Takabisha open?

Takabisha opened on 16 Jul 2011.

Who built Takabisha?

Takabisha was manufactured by Gerstlauer Amusement Rides GmbH.

How long is the ride on Takabisha?

RCDB lists a ride time of 1 minute 52 seconds over 3280.8 ft of track.

Does Takabisha launch?

Yes. RCDB lists a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that starts while the train is already moving.

How many people ride Takabisha at once?

Up to 8 per train, going by RCDB's train layout.

What restraints does Takabisha use?

RCDB lists: seatbelt, shoulder harness.

Is Takabisha good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 7 times; it tops out at 62.1 mph; the minimum height is 125 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Takabisha at Fuji-Q Highland the same as TMNT Shellraiser at Nickelodeon Universe NJ?

Both are Gerstlauer Amusement Rides GmbH Euro-Fighter / 1000 coasters on RCDB; the Fuji-Q Highland ride opened in 2011 and the Nickelodeon Universe NJ ride in 2019; Fuji-Q Highland sets 125 cm and Nickelodeon Universe NJ sets 48 in.
