Voltron Nevera

Europa-Park · Rust, Germany

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 130 cm (about 51.2 in); top speed 62.1 mph; 106.6 ft tall; 7 inversions; opened 2024.

Voltron Nevera is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Europa-Park in Rust, Germany, built by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG. It opened on 26 Apr 2024. The ride stands 106.6 ft tall, reaches 62.1 mph and runs 4,544 ft of track. It turns riders upside down 7 times.

What riding Voltron Nevera is like

With 4,544 ft of track for a 106.6 ft structure, Voltron Nevera is a long, low-slung ride: most of the time is spent carving through turns and small hills rather than on one big plunge. It goes upside down 7 times across 4,544 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2024, it is the newest of the 14 coasters we list at Europa-Park.

A rider who just meets its 130 cm minimum can also ride 12 other coasters at Europa-Park, out of 13 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is about typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Voltron Nevera sits near the middle of Europa-Park's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Euro Mir, roughly 90 m away (130 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Swiss Bob Run at about 150 m. Voltron Nevera layout, trains and ride system

Voltron Nevera starts with a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that the train passes through more than once rather than relying only on gravity from a lift. It also uses a magnetic (LSM) lift hill. RCDB also lists 1 booster section (LSM Boost) that add speed mid-course.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 7 times. Riders must be at least 130 cm (about 51.2 in) tall.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

Before you ride Voltron Nevera

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 130 cm (about 51.2 in). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 7 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Europa-Park app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Voltron Nevera?

Riders must be at least 130 cm (about 51.2 in) tall to ride Voltron Nevera, according to Europa-Park, official attraction page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Voltron Nevera?

Voltron Nevera is 106.6 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Voltron Nevera go?

Its top speed is 62.1 mph (100 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Voltron Nevera go upside down?

Yes. It has 7 inversions.

Is Voltron Nevera scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Voltron Nevera open?

Voltron Nevera opened on 26 Apr 2024.

Who built Voltron Nevera?

Voltron Nevera was manufactured by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG.

How long is the ride on Voltron Nevera?

RCDB lists a ride time of 3 minutes over 4,544 ft of track.

Does Voltron Nevera launch?

Yes. RCDB lists a linear synchronous motor (LSM) launch that the train passes through more than once.

How many people ride Voltron Nevera at once?

Each train carries 16 riders, according to RCDB. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,600 riders.

Is Voltron Nevera good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 7 times; it tops out at 62.1 mph; the minimum height is 130 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
