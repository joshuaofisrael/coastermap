Gauntlet

Magic Springs Theme and Water Park · Hot Springs, Arkansas

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 11 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 52 in (132 cm); top speed 49.7 mph; 109 ft tall; 5 inversions; opened 2004.

Gauntlet is a steel inverted roller coaster at Magic Springs Theme and Water Park in Hot Springs, Arkansas, built by Vekoma. It opened on 10 Apr 2004. The ride stands 109 ft tall, reaches 49.7 mph and runs 2260.5 ft of track. On an inverted coaster the seats hang below the track, so riders' feet dangle free with nothing beneath them. It turns riders upside down 5 times.

What riding Gauntlet is like

Gauntlet pairs a 109 ft structure with 2,260.5 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. It goes upside down 5 times across 2,260.5 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2004, it is the newest of the 3 coasters we list at Magic Springs Theme and Water Park. It is one of 22 rides on Coaster Map built to Vekoma's SLC / 689m Standard design, and the 15th of them to open. Others include DareDeviler at Canada's Wonderland, Riddler Revenge at Six Flags New England, Kong at Discovery Kingdom and Thunderhawk at Michigan's Adventure. Height rules for this model differ by park: Magic Springs sets 52 in, while Energylandia sets 140 cm, Heide Park sets 130 cm and Movie Park Germany sets 130 cm.

Its 52 in minimum is the strictest at Magic Springs. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Gauntlet sits on the south-west side of Magic Springs' coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Diamond Mine Run, roughly 170 m away (42 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Big Bad John at about 300 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 5 times. Riders must be at least 52 in (132 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

On this style of coaster, the front row has nothing in your line of sight, and the outermost seats feel the most exposed. Riders after the strongest drops pick the back row, which is pulled over each crest faster.

Before you ride Gauntlet

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 52 in (132 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 5 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Magic Springs Theme and Water Park app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Gauntlet?

Riders must be at least 52 in (132 cm) tall to ride Gauntlet, according to Magic Springs Theme and Water Park, official thrill rides page (checked 11 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Gauntlet?

Gauntlet is 109 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does Gauntlet go?

Its top speed is 49.7 mph (80 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Gauntlet go upside down?

Yes. It has 5 inversions.

Is Gauntlet scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Gauntlet open?

Gauntlet opened on 10 Apr 2004.

Who built Gauntlet?

Gauntlet was manufactured by Vekoma.

How long is the ride on Gauntlet?

RCDB lists a ride time of 1 minute 36 seconds over 2260.5 ft of track.

How does Gauntlet get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Gauntlet at once?

RCDB's train details give 20 seats per train. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 1,040 riders.

Is Gauntlet good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 5 times; it tops out at 49.7 mph; the minimum height is 52 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Gauntlet at Magic Springs the same as DareDeviler at Canada's Wonderland?

Both are Vekoma SLC / 689m Standard coasters on RCDB; the Magic Springs ride opened in 2004 and the Canada's Wonderland ride in 1995; both list a 52 in minimum; RCDB lists heights of 109 ft and 109.3 ft.
