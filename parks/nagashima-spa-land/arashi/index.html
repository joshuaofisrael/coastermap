Arashi

Nagashima Spa Land · Kuwana, Mie, Japan

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 120 cm (about 47.2 in); top speed 38 mph; 120 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2017.

Arashi is a steel wing roller coaster at Nagashima Spa Land in Kuwana, Mie, Japan, built by S&S Worldwide. It opened on 10 Mar 2017. The ride stands 120 ft tall, drops 54 ft, reaches 38 mph and runs 1,019 ft of track. On a wing coaster the seats stick out on either side of the track, so the outer riders have nothing above or below them. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Arashi is like

For a 120 ft ride, 1,019 ft of track is short: Arashi is compact and vertical, with little time between its main elements. RCDB lists a 54 ft drop against a 120 ft height, so the first drop does not fall the full height of the structure.

Opened in 2017, it is the 10th oldest of the 11 coasters we list at Nagashima Spa Land. It is one of 9 rides on Coaster Map built to S&S Worldwide's 4D Free Spin / 110 design, and the 3rd of them to open. Others include The Joker at Six Flags Great Adventure, The Joker at Six Flags Great America, The Joker at Six Flags Over Texas and Batman The Ride at Six Flags Fiesta Texas. Height rules for this model differ by park: Nagashima Spa Land sets 120 cm, while Six Flags Great Adventure sets 48 in, Six Flags Great America sets 48 in and Six Flags Over Texas sets 48 in.

A rider who just meets its 120 cm minimum can also ride 3 other coasters at Nagashima Spa Land, out of 7 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Arashi sits on the north side of Nagashima Spa Land's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Corkscrew, roughly 90 m away (110 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Double Wild Mouse at about 90 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall.

Best seat to ride

On this style of coaster, the front row has nothing in your line of sight, and the outermost seats feel the most exposed. Riders after the strongest drops pick the back row, which is pulled over each crest faster.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Arashi?

Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall to ride Arashi, according to Nagashima Spa Land, official attraction restrictions page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Arashi?

Arashi is 120 ft tall, with a drop of 54 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Arashi go?

Its top speed is 38 mph (61 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Arashi go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Arashi scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Arashi open?

Arashi opened on 10 Mar 2017.

Who built Arashi?

Arashi was manufactured by S&S Worldwide.

How long is the track on Arashi?

RCDB lists 1,019 ft (311 m) of track.

How does Arashi get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a vertical chain lift; it has no launch.

How many people ride Arashi at once?

RCDB's train details give 8 seats per train.

Is Arashi good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it has no inversions; it tops out at 38 mph; the minimum height is 120 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Arashi at Nagashima Spa Land the same as Batman The Ride at Six Flags Fiesta Texas?

Both are S&S Worldwide 4D Free Spin / 110 coasters on RCDB; the Nagashima Spa Land ride opened in 2017 and the Six Flags Fiesta Texas ride in 2015; Nagashima Spa Land sets 120 cm and Six Flags Fiesta Texas sets 48 in.
