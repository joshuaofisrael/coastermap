The Gold Coaster

Dreamworld · Coomera, Australia

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 120 cm (about 47.2 in); top speed 52.8 mph; 131.3 ft tall; 2 inversions; opened 2001.

The Gold Coaster is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Dreamworld in Coomera, Australia, built by Arrow Dynamics. It first opened on 26 Dec 2001 and was previously known as Cyclone and later Hot Wheels SideWinder. The ride stands 131.3 ft tall, reaches 52.8 mph and runs 2952.8 ft of track. The train has an onboard soundtrack. It turns riders upside down 2 times.

What riding The Gold Coaster is like

The Gold Coaster pairs a 131.3 ft structure with 2,952.8 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. It goes upside down 2 times across 2,952.8 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2001, it is the oldest of the 5 coasters we list at Dreamworld. It has run under other names (Cyclone, then Hot Wheels SideWinder), so older guides and photos may use those.

A rider who just meets its 120 cm minimum can also ride 2 other coasters at Dreamworld, out of 4 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, The Gold Coaster sits on the south-west side of Dreamworld's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Kenny's Forest Flyer, roughly 200 m away (130 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Steel Taipan at about 310 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 2 times. Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall, and no taller than 205 cm (about 80.7 in).

Best seat to ride

Pick the front row for the view and the back rows for airtime: the train drags the rear cars over every crest faster than the front.

How The Gold Coaster compares

On our intensity ranking The Gold Coaster is number 2 of 5 at Dreamworld, where 1 is the most intense. It is the tallest of the park's 3 coasters with a listed height.

Before you ride The Gold Coaster

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 120 cm (about 47.2 in). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Tall riders: the maximum is 205 cm (about 80.7 in). See can you be too tall?
  • Riders must be at least 10 years old.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 2 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Dreamworld app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for The Gold Coaster?

Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall to ride The Gold Coaster. The maximum rider height is 205 cm (about 80.7 in). Riders must be at least 10 years old, according to Dreamworld, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is The Gold Coaster?

The Gold Coaster is 131.3 ft tall, according to RCDB.

How fast does The Gold Coaster go?

Its top speed is 52.8 mph (85 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does The Gold Coaster go upside down?

Yes. It has 2 inversions.

Is The Gold Coaster scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did The Gold Coaster open?

The Gold Coaster opened on 26 Dec 2001 (previously known as Cyclone and later Hot Wheels SideWinder).

Who built The Gold Coaster?

The Gold Coaster was manufactured by Arrow Dynamics.

How long is the track on The Gold Coaster?

RCDB lists 2952.8 ft of track.

How many people ride The Gold Coaster at once?

Each train carries 24 riders, according to RCDB.

Is The Gold Coaster good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 2 times; it tops out at 52.8 mph; the minimum height is 120 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Where did The Gold Coaster operate before?

RCDB records it at Luna Park (as Big Dipper).
