The Fly

Canada's Wonderland · Vaughan, Ontario

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm), or 44 to 54 in (112 to 137 cm) with a companion; top speed 35 mph; 51.8 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 1999.

The Fly is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Canada's Wonderland in Vaughan, Ontario, built by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG. It opened on 2 May 1999. The ride stands 51.8 ft tall, drops 49.8 ft, reaches 35 mph and runs 1312.3 ft of track. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding The Fly is like

The Fly pairs a 51.8 ft structure with 1,312.3 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it.

Opened in 1999, it is the 10th oldest of the 18 coasters we list at Canada's Wonderland. It is one of 7 rides on Coaster Map built to Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG's Wilde Maus / Large Park design, and the first of them to open. Others include Coast Rider at Knott's, Apple Zapple at Kings Dominion, The Great LEGO Race at Legoland Florida and LEGO Technic Coaster at Legoland California. Height rules for this model differ by park: Canada's Wonderland sets 54 in, while Legoland Florida sets 48 in, Legoland California sets 48 in and Legoland Deutschland sets 120 cm.

Its 54 in minimum is the strictest at Canada's Wonderland, shared with 3 other coasters. Among thrill-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 48 in (122 cm), so this rule is higher than is typical for its class. Children from 44 to 54 in (112 to 137 cm) can ride with a companion, so a family can often ride together before a child is old enough to ride alone.

Going by RCDB's map positions, The Fly sits near the middle of Canada's Wonderland's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Thunder Run, roughly 80 m away (46 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is The Bat at about 100 m. How intense is it?

Moderate (thrill)

RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits riders who enjoy speed and turns but may not want the biggest drops in the park and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall, or 44 to 54 in (112 to 137 cm) with a companion.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for The Fly?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride The Fly, or 44 to 54 in (112 to 137 cm) with a companion, according to Canada's Wonderland, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is The Fly?

The Fly is 51.8 ft tall, with a drop of 49.8 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does The Fly go?

Its top speed is 35 mph (56 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does The Fly go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is The Fly scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster. Expect a lively ride that stops short of the most extreme coasters.

When did The Fly open?

The Fly opened on 2 May 1999.

Who built The Fly?

The Fly was manufactured by Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG.

How long is the ride on The Fly?

RCDB lists a ride time of 1 minute 30 seconds over 1312.3 ft of track.

How does The Fly get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride The Fly at once?

Each train carries 4 riders, according to RCDB.

Is The Fly good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as thrill; it has no inversions; it tops out at 35 mph; the minimum height is 54 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is The Fly at Canada's Wonderland the same as LEGO Technic Coaster at Legoland California?

Both are Mack Rides GmbH & Co KG Wilde Maus / Large Park coasters on RCDB; the Canada's Wonderland ride opened in 1999 and the Legoland California ride in 2001; Canada's Wonderland sets 54 in and Legoland California sets 48 in.
