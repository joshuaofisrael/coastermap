Thunder Striker

Carowinds · Charlotte, North Carolina

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 54 in (137 cm); top speed 75 mph; 232 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2010.

Thunder Striker is a steel sit-down roller coaster at Carowinds in Charlotte, North Carolina, built by Bolliger & Mabillard. It first opened on 27 Mar 2010 and was previously known as Intimidator. The ride stands 232 ft tall, drops 211 ft, reaches 75 mph and runs 5,316 ft of track. That height makes it a hyper coaster (200 to 299 ft tall). It does not turn riders upside down.

How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Carowinds rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

For the strongest airtime, sit near the back: the cars ahead pull the back of the train over each crest faster, so you leave your seat sooner on the drops. For the best view of what is coming, sit in the front row.

Seat advice is general guidance based on how this style of coaster moves, not a park rule.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Thunder Striker?

Riders must be at least 54 in (137 cm) tall to ride Thunder Striker, according to Carowinds, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Thunder Striker?

Thunder Striker is 232 ft tall, with a drop of 211 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Thunder Striker go?

Its top speed is 75 mph (121 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Thunder Striker go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Thunder Striker scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Carowinds rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Thunder Striker open?

Thunder Striker opened on 27 Mar 2010 (previously known as Intimidator).

Who built Thunder Striker?
