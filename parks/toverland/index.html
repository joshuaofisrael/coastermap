Toverland height requirements and roller coasters ranked

Sevenum, Netherlands · 5 coasters

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Toverland in Sevenum, Netherlands has 5 operating roller coasters on RCDB. Height requirements run from 120 cm (about 47.2 in) on Toos-Express to 140 cm (about 55.1 in) on Booster Bike. Riding alone, a 42-inch (107 cm) child can ride 0, a 48-inch (122 cm) child 3 and a 54-inch (137 cm) rider 4 of them.

What the coasters at Toverland are like

RCDB classes 1 coaster here as kiddie or family, 2 as thrill and 2 as extreme, which makes Toverland a thrill-heavy park for roller coasters. Extreme coasters make up 40% of its lineup, against 40% across all 210 parks on Coaster Map.

The typical (median) height requirement here is about 47.2 in, lower than the 48 in typical across the site, and 2 of the 5 coasters with a verified rule need a rider of at least 48 in (122 cm).

Of the ride types, 1 of the 5 go upside down; 1 is a wooden coaster (Troy).

The most common builder is Vekoma, with 2 of the coasters.

The longest-serving coaster in the lineup is Toos-Express, which opened in 2001.

Toverland publishes its height rules in centimetres. We show the official centimetre figure with an inch conversion, and every list on this page compares in centimetres, so a child exactly at the line counts as tall enough.

What can my child ride at Toverland? By height

At Toverland the biggest single step is reaching 48 in (122 cm), which opens up 3 more coasters than the height below. Measure at home in the shoes your child will wear, since parks measure at the ride entrance and a child even slightly under the line is turned away.

36 in (91 cm) tall

A 36-inch rider is below the ride-alone minimum on every coaster here. With a companion they can also ride Toos-Express (90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in)). Still too short for 4 coasters, the nearest being Troy at 120 cm (about 47.2 in).

42 in (107 cm) tall

A 42-inch rider is below the ride-alone minimum on every coaster here. With a companion they can also ride Toos-Express (90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in)) and Dwervelwind (100 to 120 cm (about 39.4 to 47.2 in)). Still too short for 3 coasters, the nearest being Troy at 120 cm (about 47.2 in).

48 in (122 cm) tall

A 48-inch rider meets the ride-alone minimum on 3 of 5 coasters with a verified rule: Toos-Express, Troy and Dwervelwind. Still too short for 2 coasters, the nearest being Fēnix at 132 cm (about 52 in).

54 in (137 cm) tall

A 54-inch rider meets the ride-alone minimum on 4 of 5 coasters with a verified rule, 1 more than at the height below: Fēnix. Still too short for 1 coaster, the nearest being Booster Bike at 140 cm (about 55.1 in).

Maximum heights

Some rides are built for children and have an upper limit: Fēnix (up to 195 cm (about 76.8 in)). Taller riders are turned away for restraint fit, not age.

Companion rules at Toverland

These are the rules Toverland states on its official pages for riding with a companion or other conditions. Who counts as a companion (age or height) is set by the park; check the linked source for the exact wording.

  • Toos-Express: riders 90 to 120 cm (about 35.4 to 47.2 in) tall must ride with a companion.
  • Dwervelwind: riders 100 to 120 cm (about 39.4 to 47.2 in) tall must ride with a companion.

Best coasters at Toverland for first-timers

Nervous riders usually do best working up in steps. Here is a three-step plan for Toverland, picked from our intensity ranking and the official height rules:

  1. Start here: Toos-Express, the gentlest coaster here by our intensity ranking, with no inversions (120 cm minimum; 39.3 ft tall, 37.3 mph, no inversions).
  2. Step up: Dwervelwind, a bigger ride that stops short of the park's most extreme coasters (120 cm minimum; 65.6 ft tall, 43.5 mph, no inversions).
  3. The big one: Fēnix, the most intense coaster in the park (132 cm minimum; 131.3 ft tall, 59 mph, 3 inversions, 5 g).

Sit in the middle of the train for the softest ride, and watch a few cycles from the queue first. Our best first roller coasters guide has more tips.
