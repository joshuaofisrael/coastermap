Goliath

Six Flags Great America · Gurnee, Illinois

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 48 in (122 cm); top speed 72 mph; 165 ft tall; 2 inversions; opened 2014.

Goliath is a wood sit-down roller coaster at Six Flags Great America in Gurnee, Illinois, built by Rocky Mountain Coasters. It opened on 19 Jun 2014. The ride stands 165 ft tall, drops 180 ft, reaches 72 mph and runs 3,100 ft of track. It is a wooden coaster, with a wooden track structure. It turns riders upside down 2 times.

What riding Goliath is like

Goliath pairs a 165 ft structure with 3,100 ft of track, a classic proportion that gives a clear first drop and a full layout after it. Its 180 ft drop is longer than its 165 ft height, which means the first drop falls below the level where it starts, into a trench, tunnel or down a hillside. At 85°, the steepest drop is close to vertical. It goes upside down 2 times across 3,100 ft of track, so inversions are spaced out between other elements.

Opened in 2014, it is the 13th oldest of the 16 coasters we list at Six Flags Great America.

A rider who just meets its 48 in minimum can also ride 10 other coasters at Six Flags Great America, out of 15 with verified rules. Among extreme-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 51 in (130 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Goliath sits on the south-east side of Six Flags Great America's coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Sprocket Rockets, roughly 40 m away (42 in minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is The Flash: Vertical Velocity at about 180 m. How intense is it?

Intense (extreme)

RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Great America rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale.

Who is it for?

It suits confident thrill seekers who are comfortable with big forces and riders happy to go upside down 2 times. Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

Before you ride Goliath

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 48 in (122 cm). Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.
  • Secure glasses, phones and loose items before the 2 inversions; the park posts its loose-article rules at the ride.
  • Opening times and ride status: check the Six Flags Great America app or official site on the day.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Goliath?

Riders must be at least 48 in (122 cm) tall to ride Goliath, according to Six Flags Great America, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Goliath?

Goliath is 165 ft tall, with a drop of 180 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Goliath go?

Its top speed is 72 mph (116 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Goliath go upside down?

Yes. It has 2 inversions.

Is Goliath scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as an extreme coaster; Six Flags Great America rates it 5 out of 5 on its own thrill scale. That is RCDB's most intense class.

When did Goliath open?

Goliath opened on 19 Jun 2014.

Who built Goliath?

Goliath was manufactured by Rocky Mountain Coasters.

How long is the track on Goliath?

RCDB lists 3,100 ft (945 m) of track.

How does Goliath get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Goliath at once?

RCDB's train details give 24 seats per train. RCDB lists an hourly capacity of about 600 riders.

Is Goliath good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as extreme; it goes upside down 2 times; it tops out at 72 mph; the minimum height is 48 in. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.

Is Goliath at Six Flags Great America the same as Goliath at Magic Mountain?

They share a name but are separate rides; the Six Flags Great America ride opened in 2014 and the Magic Mountain ride in 2000; both list a 48 in minimum; RCDB lists heights of 165 ft and 235 ft.
