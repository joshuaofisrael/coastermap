Timber

Walibi Rhône-Alpes · Les Avenières, France

By the Coaster Map editorial team · Last verified 10 Nov 2026 · How we verify

Height requirement: 120 cm (about 47.2 in), or 110 to 120 cm (about 43.3 to 47.2 in) with a companion; top speed 40 mph; 55.8 ft tall; 0 inversions; opened 2016.

Timber is a wood sit-down roller coaster at Walibi Rhône-Alpes in Les Avenières, France, built by Gravitykraft Corporation. It opened on 9 Apr 2016. The ride stands 55.8 ft tall, drops 54.8 ft, reaches 40 mph and runs 1,464 ft of track. It is a wooden coaster, with a wooden track structure. It does not turn riders upside down.

What riding Timber is like

At 55.8 ft tall with 1,464 ft of track, Timber balances height and length: a main drop followed by a full circuit of hills and turns.

Opened in 2016, it is the 4th oldest of the 6 coasters we list at Walibi Rhône-Alpes.

Its 120 cm minimum is the lowest of any coaster at Walibi Rhône-Alpes (tied with 3 others), which makes it a natural first coaster there. Among thrill-class coasters on Coaster Map the median ride-alone minimum is about 48 in (122 cm), so this rule is lower than is typical for its class. Children from 110 to 120 cm (about 43.3 to 47.2 in) can ride with a companion, so a family can often ride together before a child is old enough to ride alone.

Going by RCDB's map positions, Timber sits on the west side of Walibi Rhône-Alpes' coaster line-up. The nearest other coaster is Woodstock Express, roughly 40 m away (120 cm minimum), so the two are easy to ride back to back. Next closest is Generator at about 160 m. How intense is it?

Moderate (thrill)

RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster.

Who is it for?

It suits riders who enjoy speed and turns but may not want the biggest drops in the park and riders who prefer not to go upside down. Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall, or 110 to 120 cm (about 43.3 to 47.2 in) with a companion.

Best seat to ride

The back rows get pulled over each hill at speed, which gives the sharpest drops and most airtime; the front row trades some of that for a clear view of the track ahead.

How Timber compares

On our intensity ranking Timber is number 4 of 6 at Walibi Rhône-Alpes, where 1 is the most intense. It is the 4th tallest of the park's 5 coasters with a listed height.

If it sounds like too much, the next gentler coaster here is Woodstock Express (no inversions; 120 cm minimum). Ready for more? The next step up is Mahuka (59.1 ft tall, 41.6 mph, 3 inversions; 120 cm minimum).

  • Measure first: the ride-alone minimum is 120 cm (about 47.2 in), and riders from 110 to 120 cm (about 43.3 to 47.2 in) need a companion. Height is checked at the entrance, so ask the ride team before you queue if your child is close.

Frequently asked questions

What is the height requirement for Timber?

Riders must be at least 120 cm (about 47.2 in) tall to ride Timber, or 110 to 120 cm (about 43.3 to 47.2 in) with a companion. Riders 110 to 120 cm must be accompanied by an adult, according to Walibi Rhône-Alpes, official ride page (checked 10 Nov 2026). Requirements can change, so confirm at the ride entrance.

How tall is Timber?

Timber is 55.8 ft tall, with a drop of 54.8 ft, according to RCDB.

How fast does Timber go?

Its top speed is 40 mph (64 km/h), according to RCDB.

Does Timber go upside down?

No. It has no inversions.

Is Timber scary?

It depends on the rider, but on intensity: RCDB classes it as a thrill coaster. Expect a lively ride that stops short of the most extreme coasters.

When did Timber open?

Timber opened on 9 Apr 2016.

Who built Timber?

Timber was manufactured by Gravitykraft Corporation.

How long is the track on Timber?

RCDB lists 1,464 ft (446 m) of track.

How does Timber get up its first hill?

RCDB lists a chain lift hill; it has no launch.

How many people ride Timber at once?

Up to 12 per train, going by RCDB's train layout.

Is Timber good for first-time riders?

On the numbers: RCDB classes it as thrill; it has no inversions; it tops out at 40 mph; the minimum height is 120 cm. Most first-timers start with a family-class ride with no inversions, so this is better as a step up than a first coaster.
