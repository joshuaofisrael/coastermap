Height requirements

From official park pages: sixflags.com ride pages for Cedar Point, Six Flags Magic Mountain, Kings Island, Knott's Berry Farm, Six Flags Great Adventure, Six Flags Great America, Six Flags Over Texas, Six Flags Over Georgia, Carowinds, Kings Dominion, Canada's Wonderland, Six Flags Fiesta Texas, Six Flags New England, Six Flags Discovery Kingdom, California's Great America, Dorney Park, Six Flags Darien Lake, Frontier City, Six Flags Mexico; the Enchanted Parks ride pages for Worlds of Fun, Valleyfair, Michigan's Adventure and Mid America Adventure; disneyworld.disney.go.com attraction pages for Magic Kingdom, EPCOT, Disney's Hollywood Studios and Disney's Animal Kingdom; Disneyland Resort attraction pages on disneyparks.disney.go.com for Disneyland Park and Disney California Adventure Park; the Busch Gardens Tampa Bay, Busch Gardens Williamsburg and SeaWorld Orlando roller coaster pages and ride pages, and the SeaWorld San Diego and SeaWorld San Antonio rides pages; dollywood.com, silverdollarcity.com, kennywood.com, lakecompounce.com and holidayworld.com ride pages; and, for Universal Studios Hollywood, the park's official Guide for Rider Safety and Accessibility. Outside North America we use each park's own official pages or guides: Europa-Park, Phantasialand, Efteling, Liseberg, Fuji-Q Highland and Shanghai Disneyland attraction pages; the Alton Towers and Pleasure Beach Resort (Blackpool) height restriction pages; Thorpe Park's official Ride Restrictions Guide; PortAventura World's height requirement pages for PortAventura Park and Ferrari Land; and Tokyo Disney Resort's official FAQ on attractions with height requirements. These parks set their rules in centimetres, so we show the official centimetre figure first with an approximate inch conversion, and the height checker compares your child's height against the rule in its own unit, so there is no rounding error at the line. Where a park adds an age rule or a companion age, we note it on the coaster page. Hersheypark groups rides by height category (for example 42 to 48 in) and its ride pages do not print the numbers, so for Hersheypark we use its official 2026 roller coaster guide on hersheypa.com and list the lowest height category allowed to ride. Where a park sets a companion rule (a shorter child may ride with someone older), we show both heights; companion age rules vary by park, so check at the ride. Universal Orlando's own ride pages could not be read by our checker, so for Epic Universe, Islands of Adventure and Universal Studios Florida we use Visit Orlando's roller coaster guide (Orlando's official tourism organisation, updated 1 Jun 2026) and will switch to Universal's pages when we can confirm them. The Barnstormer's requirement is also from Visit Orlando. All were checked 10 Nov 2026. Ride stats and positions

Height, drop, speed, length, inversions, drop angle, g-force, opening date, manufacturer, model, type and map coordinates come from each coaster's RCDB entry. Our park lists include operating coasters only, as listed on RCDB on 10 Nov 2026. Dates of a ride's first opening follow RCDB; where a park dates a rebuilt version differently, the coaster page notes it.

Intensity, who it suits and best seat

Intensity labels use RCDB's scale (kiddie, family, thrill, extreme) and, where the park publishes one, the park's own thrill rating. "Who it suits" is drawn from those labels, the height rules and the number of inversions. Seat advice is general guidance based on how each style of coaster moves (for example, back rows crest drops faster); it is not a park rule.
